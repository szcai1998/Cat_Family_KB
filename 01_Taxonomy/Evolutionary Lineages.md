# Evolutionary Lineages of Felidae

The history of the cat family is a story of radiation and adaptation across millions of years.  Fossil and genetic evidence indicate that the felid lineage diverged from other carnivores during the Oligocene.  The **first major split within Felidae** occurred more than **10 million years** ago when the ancestral cats separated into the sub‑families **Pantherinae** and **Felinae**【844804255495323†L266-L279】.

### Early divergence

- **Pantherinae**: The big‑cat lineage, including the ancestors of tigers, lions, jaguars, leopards and clouded leopards, diverged first.  Genetic analyses suggest that the clouded leopards (*Neofelis*) branched off from the other pantherines over six million years ago【452191132347828†L152-L182】.
- **Felinae**: After the split from Pantherinae, the small‑cat lineage continued to radiate.  Modern felinae genera (e.g. *Felis*, *Puma*, *Acinonyx*, *Lynx*) evolved primarily during the last **1–3 million years**【844804255495323†L266-L279】.

### Genera diversification

- **Panthera** – The genus containing the five “true” big cats evolved in Asia around six million years ago and later spread to Africa and the Americas.  Tigers and snow leopards are more closely related to each other than either is to the lion or jaguar.
- **Neofelis** – The clouded leopards represent an ancient branch that diverged early in the Pantherinae line.  Their long canine teeth and exceptional climbing abilities hint at a morphology intermediate between sabre‑toothed cats and modern pantherines【452191132347828†L152-L182】.
- **Felinae genera** – The small‑cat lineages diversified to fill a variety of ecological niches.  Cheetahs evolved speed for open‑plain hunting【281884566239886†L57-L66】; cougars and lynxes adapted to various forests and mountains【623734751930192†L32-L39】【200188625492883†L70-L74】; servals and caracals specialise in long legs and large ears for grassland hunting【300802010599608†L50-L60】【774389635293037†L70-L81】; ocelots and their relatives evolved in the tropical forests of the Americas【999739901386276†L96-L104】; and Pallas’s cats developed thick fur for cold steppes【448592063858525†L159-L170】.

### Hyoid bone and vocalisation

The morphology of the hyoid apparatus played a critical role in felid diversification.  Pantherine cats retained a **flexible hyoid** that permits roaring, whereas felines evolved a **rigid hyoid** that allows purring but not roaring【844804255495323†L246-L260】【889426918678370†L42-L50】.  This anatomical difference underpins the division between the big‑cat and small‑cat lineages.

Understanding these lineages helps explain why certain cats share traits even when they live on different continents.  The species pages provide more detail on how each cat fits into this evolutionary framework.
