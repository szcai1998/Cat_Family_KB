# Leopard vs Jaguar

Leopards (*Panthera pardus*) and jaguars (*Panthera onca*) are often confused because of their spotted coats.  This page highlights the key differences between these big cats.

## Coat pattern

- **Jaguar rosettes**: Jaguar spots are large rosettes with **central black dots** inside each rosette【103081960674531†L200-L215】.  The rosettes are spaced further apart on a golden background.  Melanistic jaguars appear almost entirely black but still have faint rosettes.
- **Leopard rosettes**: Leopards have smaller rosettes without central dots; the pattern is denser and covers the entire body【103081960674531†L200-L215】.  Leopards in wetter forests tend to have more open rosettes, whereas those in arid regions are more densely spotted.

## Body build

- **Jaguar**: Stocky and muscular with a broad head and powerful jaws.  Jaguars have the **strongest bite force relative to body size** among big cats【677992921894485†L30-L57】.  They are excellent swimmers and often hunt in water.
- **Leopard**: More slender and lightly built.  Leopards are extremely agile climbers and frequently drag prey up trees.  They are built for stealth and quick bursts of speed【103081960674531†L219-L233】.

## Size

Jaguars are generally larger; adult males can weigh over 100 kg and reach 170 cm in body length【898441706688837†L119-L124】.  Leopards usually weigh 60–90 kg and are slightly shorter.

## Distribution

- **Jaguar**: Found exclusively in the **Americas**, from Mexico through Central America into South America【103081960674531†L260-L266】.
- **Leopard**: Widely distributed across **Africa and Asia**【103081960674531†L260-L266】.

## Behaviour and ecology

Jaguars inhabit tropical forests, wetlands and grasslands.  They are solitary and hunt a wide range of prey, including caimans and turtles.  Leopards are adaptable to many habitats, from rainforests to deserts, and are also solitary.  Leopards often cache kills in trees to avoid scavengers, whereas jaguars rarely do so.

## Conservation

Both species are threatened by habitat loss and hunting.  Jaguars are classified as **Near Threatened**, while leopards are **Vulnerable**.  Conservation efforts include creating wildlife corridors and reducing conflict with livestock owners.
