# Lynx vs Caracal vs Serval

These three medium‑sized cats—lynx, caracal and serval—belong to the Felinae sub‑family but occupy different ecological niches and display distinct adaptations.

## Ear features

- **Eurasian lynx**: possesses conspicuous **black ear tufts**, thought to enhance hearing or act as visual signals【200188625492883†L41-L66】.
- **Caracal**: has **very long black ear tufts** (up to 5 cm) that may help camouflage or communicate【774389635293037†L63-L67】.
- **Serval**: lacks ear tufts but has **enormous, rounded ears** that are the largest relative to head size of any cat, providing acute hearing【300802010599608†L57-L59】.

## Leg length and body form

- **Lynx**: long legs and wide, fur‑covered paws act like snowshoes, enabling movement on snow【200188625492883†L70-L74】.  Body is stocky with a short tail.
- **Caracal**: long legs and sleek, muscular body make it the heaviest and fastest of Africa’s small cats【774389635293037†L50-L55】.
- **Serval**: has **extremely long legs and a long neck**, giving it a “giraffe‑cat” appearance【300802010599608†L50-L53】.  Body is slender with small head.

## Habitat and hunting

- **Lynx**: inhabits temperate and boreal forests across Europe and Asia【200188625492883†L129-L142】.  Hunts by stealth and pounces on prey such as deer and hares【200188625492883†L81-L99】.
- **Caracal**: lives in savannas, semi‑deserts and woodlands across Africa and into India【774389635293037†L70-L81】.  Leaps up to 3 m to snatch birds in flight【774389635293037†L70-L81】.
- **Serval**: frequents wetlands and savannas near streams in central and southern Africa【300802010599608†L70-L74】.  Uses a “wait and pounce” strategy; can jump 2.7 m straight up to catch birds and uses extra‑long legs to reach into rodent burrows【300802010599608†L87-L94】.

## Conservation

All three species are currently listed as **Least Concern**, though regional populations may be threatened.  Caracals and servals may come into conflict with farmers when they prey on small livestock【774389635293037†L123-L129】, and lynx populations declined in Western Europe before reintroduction【200188625492883†L129-L142】.
