# Big Cats vs Small Cats

The cat family divides into two living sub‑families: **Pantherinae** (big cats) and **Felinae** (small cats).  Although people often call any large wild cat a “big cat,” biologists use specific anatomical criteria to define the Pantherinae.  This page contrasts the key features of the two branches.

## Vocalisation

- **Big cats**: Lions, tigers, leopards, jaguars, snow leopards and clouded leopards have a **flexible hyoid bone** that allows them to **roar**.  They cannot purr continuously because the larynx does not vibrate in the same way【844804255495323†L246-L260】.
- **Small cats**: Members of Felinae, including the cheetah, cougar, lynx, caracal, serval, ocelot and domestic cat, have a **rigid hyoid**.  They **purr** when both inhaling and exhaling but cannot produce true roars【889426918678370†L42-L50】.

## Body size and build

- **Big cats** are generally larger and heavier.  The Siberian tiger can exceed 660 pounds【736746662787535†L160-L166】, while lions and jaguars commonly weigh over 100 kg.  They have robust skulls and powerful limb musculature for tackling large prey.
- **Small cats** vary greatly in size—from the 2 kg domestic cat to the 90 kg cougar【889426918678370†L54-L67】.  Many are slender or lightly built and specialise in smaller prey.

## Claws and hunting style

- **Big cats** typically have retractable claws used for gripping prey and climbing, though they rarely rely on speed alone.  They stalk and ambush large ungulates.
- **Small cats** display diverse specialisations.  Cheetahs have **non‑retractable claws** and long legs for sprinting at high speed【281884566239886†L57-L66】, while servals combine long legs and huge ears to hunt rodents in tall grass【300802010599608†L50-L60】.  Many small cats are accomplished climbers and swimmers, such as the ocelot【999739901386276†L88-L95】.

## Social structure

- **Big cats**: Most big cats are solitary, but lions form social groups called **prides** in which related females raise cubs cooperatively【412797920315225†L86-L104】.
- **Small cats**: Small cats are predominantly solitary.  Cougars and ocelots maintain territories that overlap those of several females【999739901386276†L131-L139】; domestic cats may form loose colonies around food sources.

## Examples

- **Big‑cat species:** [[Siberian Tiger]], [[African Lion]], [[Jaguar]], [[African Leopard]], [[Snow Leopard]], [[Clouded Leopard]].
- **Small‑cat species:** [[Cheetah]], [[Cougar - Puma]], [[Eurasian Lynx]], [[Caracal]], [[Serval]], [[Ocelot]], [[Pallas’s Cat]], [[Domestic Cat]].

Understanding these differences helps clarify why cheetahs and cougars—though large in size—are classified among the “small cats.”  For more on the evolutionary split see [[Evolutionary Lineages]].
