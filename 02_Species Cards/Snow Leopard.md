# Snow Leopard

## Scientific classification

- **Sub‑family:** [[Pantherinae]]
- **Genus:** *Panthera*
- **Species:** *Panthera uncia*

## Physical characteristics

Snow leopards have a thick, pale grey coat flecked with rosettes and spots.  Their fur ranges from **5 cm to 12 cm** in length, providing insulation against extreme cold【195888652515351†L95-L101】.  A long, bushy tail (80–105 cm) aids balance on steep terrain and can be wrapped around the body for warmth【195888652515351†L95-L101】.  They have relatively short forelimbs, long hind limbs and large, fur‑covered paws that function as natural **snowshoes**【195888652515351†L95-L101】.  Although part of the big‑cat sub‑family, snow leopards **cannot roar**; instead they emit a high‑pitched yowl【195888652515351†L129-L131】.

## Habitat and range

Snow leopards inhabit the high mountains of **Central and South Asia**.  They are typically found at elevations between **3,000 m and 4,500 m** on rocky slopes and alpine meadows【195888652515351†L118-L122】.  Their range spans the Himalayas, the Tibetan Plateau and mountain ranges in Mongolia, China, India, Pakistan and Afghanistan.

## Behaviour and diet

These solitary cats are crepuscular, active at dawn and dusk.  They prey on wild sheep and goats, marmots, pikas and birds.  Snow leopards are capable of leaping up to 15 metres and use rugged terrain to their advantage.  They mark territory with scrapes and sprays and communicate by scent and vocalisations.

## Special traits

- **Cold‑climate specialist:** dense fur, woolly belly and long tail insulate them in temperatures that can drop well below freezing【195888652515351†L95-L101】.
- **Mountain agility:** long hind legs and large paws enable them to ascend and descend steep cliffs【195888652515351†L95-L101】.
- **Non‑roaring big cat:** although genetically part of *Panthera*, snow leopards cannot roar【195888652515351†L129-L131】.
- **Close relation to tigers:** genetic studies show snow leopards are more closely related to tigers than to leopards【195888652515351†L138-L142】.

## Conservation status

Snow leopards are listed as **Vulnerable**.  Their populations are threatened by habitat loss, retaliatory killings from livestock predation and illegal trade.  Conservation programmes focus on community involvement and transboundary cooperation across their wide range.
