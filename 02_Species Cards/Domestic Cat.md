# Domestic Cat

## Scientific classification

- **Sub‑family:** [[Felinae]]
- **Genus:** *Felis*
- **Species:** *Felis catus* (or *Felis silvestris catus*)

## Origins and domestication

All domestic cats descend from the African wildcat **(*Felis silvestris lybica*)**.  Archaeological and genetic evidence indicates that **domestication began in the Near East around 10,000 years ago**, when early farmers in the Fertile Crescent stored grain that attracted rodents; wildcats preyed on these rodents and gradually adapted to human settlements【925365723471490†L77-L99】.  Cats were later venerated in ancient Egypt, where they were depicted in art and protected by law.  As seafaring spread, cats were carried on ships to control rodents, facilitating their global dispersal【925365723471490†L103-L107】.

## Physical characteristics

Domestic cats are small felids, typically weighing between 2 and 7 kg.  Their body length ranges from 30–40 cm with tails about half as long.  Coat colours and patterns vary widely due to selective breeding and natural variation.  Unlike pantherine cats, domestic cats have a **rigid hyoid bone** and can **purr continuously**, but they cannot produce a true roar【889426918678370†L42-L50】.

## Behaviour and ecology

Domestic cats are largely solitary but are capable of forming loose colonies, especially around abundant food sources.  They retain many behaviours of wildcats: stalking and pouncing on small prey such as rodents, birds and insects.  Cats communicate through vocalisations (purring, meows, growls), body language and scent marking.  They possess acute hearing and night vision but rely less on smell than wild canids.

## Special traits

- **Commensal relationship:** domestic cats live in close association with humans yet remain self‑sufficient hunters.【925365723471490†L77-L99】
- **Purring:** a rigid hyoid bone enables cats to generate a continuous purr when inhaling and exhaling【889426918678370†L42-L50】.
- **Phenotypic diversity:** through domestication and selective breeding, over 60 recognised breeds exhibit a range of coat types, colours and body forms.

## Conservation status

The domestic cat is not a wild species and is not assessed by the IUCN.  However, feral and free‑ranging cats can impact native wildlife through predation.  Responsible pet ownership includes spaying/neutering and keeping cats indoors or in enclosures.
