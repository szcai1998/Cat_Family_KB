# Cougar – Puma (Mountain Lion)

## Scientific classification

- **Sub‑family:** [[Felinae]]
- **Genus:** *Puma*
- **Species:** *Puma concolor*

## Physical characteristics

Cougars, also known as pumas, mountain lions or catamounts, are **large members of the small‑cat branch**.  Adults measure **1.8–2.4 m in length** (including the tail) and may weigh up to **90 kg**【889426918678370†L54-L67】.  Their coats are uniform tawny or reddish‑brown without rosettes or stripes, and the tail ends in a black tip.  Cougars have a small head relative to body size, powerful shoulders and long hind legs for jumping.  They possess retractable claws and carnassial teeth adapted for shearing meat【623734751930192†L84-L87】.

## Habitat and range

Cougars are the **most widely distributed cat in the Western Hemisphere**, ranging from the Yukon in Canada through the United States and Central America to the southern Andes of Chile and Argentina【623734751930192†L32-L39】.  They occupy diverse habitats including forests, mountains, deserts and wetlands.  Their adaptability allows them to persist near human settlements.

## Behaviour and diet

Cougars are solitary and mainly crepuscular.  They are ambush predators, using stealth to stalk prey before pouncing.  Their diet consists primarily of deer, elk and other ungulates but also includes smaller mammals.  Cougars are strong leapers and climbers.  They **cannot roar** because, like other Felinae cats, they possess a rigid hyoid bone【889426918678370†L42-L50】; instead they purr, hiss and growl.  Peak activity occurs at dawn and dusk【623734751930192†L110-L113】.

## Special traits

- **Many names:** cougars are known by more than 40 common names across the Americas【889426918678370†L54-L67】.
- **Wide range:** they occupy the largest geographic range of any wild terrestrial mammal in the Western Hemisphere【623734751930192†L32-L39】.
- **Puma lineage:** along with the cheetah, cougars belong to the Puma lineage of Felinae.  They are the **second largest cat in the Americas** after the jaguar【889426918678370†L54-L67】.

## Conservation status

The cougar is listed as **Least Concern** globally but some regional populations are endangered.  Major threats include habitat loss, fragmentation, vehicular collisions and persecution from livestock ranchers.  In the eastern United States the cougar is believed extirpated.
