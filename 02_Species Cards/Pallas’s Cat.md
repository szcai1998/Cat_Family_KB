# Pallas’s Cat (Manul)

## Scientific classification

- **Sub‑family:** [[Felinae]]
- **Genus:** *Otocolobus*
- **Species:** *Otocolobus manul*

## Physical characteristics

Pallas’s cats, also called manuls, have the **densest fur of any cat**【448592063858525†L159-L170】.  Their grey winter coats insulate them when temperatures in the mountains drop below −58 °F (−50 °C) and are shed in summer【448592063858525†L166-L170】.  They are about the size of a domestic cat but appear much stockier due to a thick layer of fat【448592063858525†L174-L176】.  Pallas’s cats have **short legs** and a distinctive flattened face with small foreheads; their eyes sit high on the skull, which may aid vision when lying flat in snow【448592063858525†L174-L183】.  A **bushy tail** helps keep their feet warm【448592063858525†L188-L190】.

## Habitat and range

These cats inhabit the rocky hills, steppes and montane grasslands of the **Central Asian steppe**, from Iran and Mongolia to western China【448592063858525†L161-L164】.  They thrive in harsh climates with extreme temperature swings and little cover.  They avoid deep snow and prefer areas with rocky crevices for dens.

## Behaviour and diet

Pallas’s cats are solitary **ambush predators**.  They stay close to the ground, using their low profile and dense coat to blend into rocky surroundings.  When crossing open plains they lie flat to avoid detection【448592063858525†L193-L205】.  Their diet consists mainly of small mammals such as pikas, voles and mice; a single adult can eat up to **five** rodents in a day【448592063858525†L215-L216】.  They also take small birds and insects.  Pallas’s cats do **not meow**; instead they honk, growl and chirp, and their excited call resembles a small dog’s yelp【448592063858525†L218-L223】.

## Special traits

- **Extreme climate adaptation:** dense fur and a stocky build allow survival in temperatures ranging from −50 °C to 32 °C【448592063858525†L159-L170】.
- **Stumpy‑eared name:** the genus name *Otocolobus* comes from Greek “oto-” (ear) and “colobus” meaning “cut short,” referring to their small, rounded ears【448592063858525†L225-L230】.
- **Low‑profile camouflage:** when threatened, Pallas’s cats flatten themselves against the ground to blend in with rocks or snow【448592063858525†L193-L205】.
- **Unique vocalisations:** they honk and growl rather than meow【448592063858525†L218-L223】.

## Conservation status

Pallas’s cats are classified as **Near Threatened** on the IUCN Red List【448592063858525†L245-L247】.  Populations appear to be declining due to illegal hunting, habitat loss and accidental poisoning【448592063858525†L242-L249】.  Because these cats are elusive and occupy remote areas, accurate population estimates are difficult.  Captive Pallas’s cats remain wild and are not suitable as pets【448592063858525†L234-L238】.
