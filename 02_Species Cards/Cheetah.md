# Cheetah

## Scientific classification

- **Sub‑family:** [[Felinae]]
- **Genus:** *Acinonyx*
- **Species:** *Acinonyx jubatus*

## Physical characteristics

Cheetahs are built for speed.  Their slender bodies, **long legs**, flexible spine and deep chest enable rapid acceleration and sustained sprints.  They can reach speeds of **up to 70 mph (112 km/h)**, making them the fastest land animals【281884566239886†L57-L66】.  Unlike other cats, cheetahs have **non‑retractable claws** that provide traction, and their tail acts as a rudder for balance at high speeds【281884566239886†L57-L66】.  Distinctive black **“tear marks”** run from the inner corners of their eyes down to the mouth, reducing glare and aiding in focus on prey【281884566239886†L57-L66】.

## Habitat and range

Cheetahs occur in open grasslands and savannas across sub‑Saharan Africa, with a tiny remnant population in Iran.  They prefer areas with good visibility and abundant medium‑sized ungulates.  Historically they ranged through India and the Middle East but are now extinct in much of their former Asian range.

## Behaviour and diet

These cats hunt **by day**, particularly in the early morning and late afternoon【281884566239886†L69-L81】.  Cheetahs rely on vision rather than smell to find prey and use their incredible acceleration to outrun antelope such as gazelles.  Hunts usually last less than a minute.  After catching their prey, cheetahs must eat quickly before larger predators arrive.  Females are solitary except when raising cubs; males may form small coalitions.

## Special traits

- **Speed specialist:** the cheetah’s musculoskeletal adaptations enable unmatched land speed【281884566239886†L57-L66】.
- **Non‑retractable claws:** provide traction during high‑speed pursuit【281884566239886†L57-L66】.
- **Silent purring cat:** cheetahs lack the flexible hyoid necessary for roaring and instead purr; they are taxonomically members of Felinae【889426918678370†L42-L50】.
- **Tear marks:** black facial stripes help cut sun glare and may be used for communication【281884566239886†L57-L66】.

## Conservation status

Cheetahs are listed as **Vulnerable**.  An estimated 7,000 adults remain.  Habitat loss, human–wildlife conflict and illegal wildlife trade threaten populations.  High cub mortality and genetic bottlenecks also hinder recovery.  Conservation strategies include protected areas, community programmes and translocation.
