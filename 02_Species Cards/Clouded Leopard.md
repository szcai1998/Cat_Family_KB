# Clouded Leopard

## Scientific classification

- **Sub‑family:** [[Pantherinae]]
- **Genus:** *Neofelis*
- **Species:** *Neofelis nebulosa*

## Physical characteristics

Clouded leopards are medium‑sized cats with a striking coat of large, cloud‑shaped blotches edged in black, giving them their common name.  They have proportionally **longer canine teeth** than any other living cat, reminiscent of sabre‑toothed cats【452191132347828†L152-L182】.  Their limbs are short and robust, and their paws have specialised pads that provide grip on branches.  The tail is long and helps with balance.

## Habitat and range

These elusive cats inhabit the **forest canopies of Southeast Asia**, including the Himalayan foothills, southern China, Thailand, Myanmar, Malaysia and Borneo.  They prefer dense tropical and subtropical forests and spend much of their time in trees.

## Behaviour and diet

Clouded leopards are primarily nocturnal and arboreal.  They feed on monkeys, birds, small deer and rodents.  Exceptional **climbers**, they can descend trees head‑first thanks to flexible anklebones【452191132347828†L152-L182】.  Unlike other Pantherinae species, clouded leopards **cannot roar or purr**; their vocalisations include growls, hisses and mews【452191132347828†L152-L182】.

## Special traits

- **Ancient lineage:** genetic studies indicate that clouded leopards diverged from other pantherine cats more than **six million years ago**, making them an early branch of the big‑cat family【452191132347828†L152-L182】.
- **Intermediate morphology:** clouded leopards display features reminiscent of both big and small cats, such as extremely long canine teeth and a lack of roaring ability【452191132347828†L152-L182】.
- **Arboreal ability:** their flexible ankles allow them to climb head‑first down trees and hang upside down from branches, a rare skill among cats【452191132347828†L152-L182】.

## Conservation status

Clouded leopards are listed as **Vulnerable**.  They face threats from deforestation, habitat fragmentation and poaching for their pelts and teeth.  Conservation efforts aim to preserve remaining forest habitats and establish wildlife corridors.
