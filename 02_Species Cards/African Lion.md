# African Lion

## Scientific classification

- **Sub‑family:** [[Pantherinae]]
- **Genus:** *Panthera*
- **Species:** *Panthera leo*

## Physical characteristics

African lions are the **second‑largest cats**, surpassed only by tigers【412797920315225†L67-L97】.  Adult males typically weigh 330–550 pounds (150–250 kg) and are distinguished by their **mane**, a ruff of hair around the head and neck that varies in colour and size.  Females lack manes and are slightly smaller.  Lions have tawny coats with lighter underbellies; cubs display rosettes that fade with age.

## Habitat and range

Historically lions ranged across Africa, the Middle East and south‑central Asia, but today **their stronghold is sub‑Saharan Africa**.  They inhabit grasslands, savannas and open woodlands where large herbivores are abundant.  A small population of Asiatic lions persists in India’s Gir Forest (not covered in this base).

## Behaviour and diet

Lions are unique among cats in their **social structure**.  They live in **prides** consisting of related females, their offspring and a coalition of males【412797920315225†L86-L104】.  Females cooperatively hunt medium to large ungulates such as zebras, wildebeest and buffalo, while males defend the territory and often take the first share of a kill.  A lion’s roar can be heard up to **five miles** (8 km) away【412797920315225†L67-L97】, serving to advertise territorial boundaries and communicate within the pride.

## Special traits

- **Social living:** lions are the only truly social cats, forming cohesive prides【412797920315225†L86-L104】.
- **Powerful roar:** their vocalisations can carry for miles and intimidate rivals【412797920315225†L67-L97】.
- **Sexual dimorphism:** males sport manes, signalling health and deterring competitors.

## Conservation status

The African lion is listed as **Vulnerable** on the IUCN Red List.  Populations have declined due to habitat loss, human–wildlife conflict and reduction of prey.  Community‑based conservation and protected areas are key to ensuring their survival.
