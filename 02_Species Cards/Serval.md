# Serval

## Scientific classification

- **Sub‑family:** [[Felinae]]
- **Genus:** *Leptailurus*
- **Species:** *Leptailurus serval*

## Physical characteristics

Servals are slender, medium‑sized cats with **very long legs**, a **long neck** and a relatively small head, earning them the nickname “giraffe cat.”  Their tawny coats bear both black **lines and spots**, while the belly is white【300802010599608†L50-L53】.  Servals possess the **largest ears relative to head size** of any cat; if humans had ears in the same proportion, they would be the size of dinner plates【300802010599608†L57-L59】.  Adults weigh up to 18 kg (40 lb)【300802010599608†L50-L60】.

## Habitat and range

Servals live in **savannas and wetlands** of central and southern Africa, particularly near streams and rivers where tall grasses provide cover【300802010599608†L70-L74】.  Unlike many cats, servals are comfortable in water and will wade or swim when hunting.  Their range overlaps with that of caracals and leopards.

## Behaviour and diet

These cats use a **“wait and see”** hunting strategy: a serval crouches in tall grass at dawn or dusk, listening for prey with its huge ears before launching a sudden pounce【300802010599608†L80-L85】.  Servals primarily eat rodents but also take birds, reptiles, frogs, crabs and insects【300802010599608†L87-L97】.  They can **leap more than 9 feet (2.7 m)** straight up to snatch birds in flight【300802010599608†L87-L90】.  Their ultrasonic hearing allows them to detect the high‑pitched calls of rodents【300802010599608†L91-L93】, and they may reach a long leg into burrows to pull out prey【300802010599608†L90-L94】.  Servals are largely solitary; males and females have overlapping ranges but meet only to mate.

## Special traits

- **Longest legs:** relative to body size, servals have the longest legs of any cat, aiding their high leaps【300802010599608†L50-L53】.
- **Huge ears:** the oversized ears provide acute hearing for locating prey in tall grass【300802010599608†L57-L59】.
- **Versatile hunter:** combines stalking, pouncing, leaping and fishing techniques; considered one of the most successful hunters among felids【300802010599608†L96-L97】.

## Conservation status

The serval is listed as **Least Concern**, though some subspecies, such as the North African serval (*L. s. constantinus*), are endangered【300802010599608†L129-L133】.  Threats include habitat loss, hunting for pelts and the exotic pet trade.  Hybridisation with domestic cats has produced the “savanna cat,” which is banned in some countries【300802010599608†L139-L143】.
