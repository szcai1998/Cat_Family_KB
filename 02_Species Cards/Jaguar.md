# Jaguar

## Scientific classification

- **Sub‑family:** [[Pantherinae]]
- **Genus:** *Panthera*
- **Species:** *Panthera onca*

## Physical characteristics

Jaguars are the **largest cats of the Americas** and the **third‑largest cats in the world** after tigers and lions【898441706688837†L119-L124】.  Males typically weigh 100–120 kg and can be up to 170 cm long (head‑body length)【898441706688837†L119-L124】.  They have a robust build, broad head and powerful jaws.  Their golden coat is covered with distinctive **rosettes** that often enclose a central black spot—an easy way to distinguish jaguars from leopards【898441706688837†L135-L139】.  Melanistic jaguars appear almost entirely black and are colloquially called “black panthers.”

## Habitat and range

Jaguars inhabit a variety of environments across **Central and South America**, from tropical rainforests and wetlands to dry scrub and grasslands.  Historically their range extended into the southern United States; today the northernmost breeding population is in Mexico.  Jaguars are strong swimmers and often live near rivers and wetlands.

## Behaviour and diet

Jaguars are solitary and primarily crepuscular.  They are opportunistic predators, taking prey ranging from fish and reptiles to peccaries, deer and caimans.  Jaguars are exceptional swimmers and are known to hunt in water.  Their bite force is the **strongest relative to body size among big cats**, enabling them to crush bones and shells【677992921894485†L30-L57】.  Jaguars typically kill by biting directly through the skull of their prey.

## Special traits

- **Powerful bite:** the jaguar’s jaw muscles and short, broad skull confer a bite force that, relative to its size, exceeds that of other big cats【677992921894485†L30-L57】.
- **Central‑spotted rosettes:** jaguar rosettes contain central dots whereas leopard rosettes are solid【898441706688837†L135-L139】.
- **Aquatic prowess:** jaguars are strong swimmers and comfortable in water.

## Conservation status

Jaguars are listed as **Near Threatened**.  Habitat loss, fragmentation and poaching for their pelts continue to reduce populations.  Conservation initiatives focus on creating biological corridors across the Americas and reducing conflict with ranchers.
