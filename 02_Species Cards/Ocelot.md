# Ocelot

## Scientific classification

- **Sub‑family:** [[Felinae]]
- **Genus:** *Leopardus*
- **Species:** *Leopardus pardalis*

## Physical characteristics

Ocelots are medium‑sized spotted cats that resemble miniature jaguars.  **Females weigh 14.5–25 pounds and males 15–34 pounds (7–15.5 kg)**【999739901386276†L61-L66】.  The body length ranges from **29 to 39 inches** (73–100 cm) with a tail 10–16 inches (25–41 cm) long【999739901386276†L61-L66】.  Their fur is golden to tawny, covered with a striking pattern of **rosettes, stripes and spots**【999739901386276†L70-L73】.  The belly is cream or white.  Ocelots have rounded ears with white spots on the back and large, expressive eyes adapted for night vision【999739901386276†L76-L81】.

## Habitat and range

Ocelots inhabit **dense forests, mangrove swamps, savannas and thorn scrub** across a broad range from **southern Texas to northern Argentina**【999739901386276†L96-L104】.  They prefer areas with heavy cover and proximity to water.  Though once found in parts of the United States, their U.S. range is now restricted to a small population in Texas.

## Behaviour and diet

Ocelots are **nocturnal**, using sharp vision and hearing to hunt at night【999739901386276†L76-L93】.  Their diet consists mainly of small rodents but also includes birds, snakes, lizards, young deer, rabbits, fish and crabs【999739901386276†L88-L94】.  Ocelots are strong climbers and swimmers and may hunt in trees or water.  They are solitary; males maintain territories that overlap several females’ ranges, marking them with scent【999739901386276†L131-L139】.

## Special traits

- **Striking coat pattern:** a complex pattern of rosettes and stripes provides excellent camouflage in dappled forest light【999739901386276†L70-L73】.
- **Excellent night vision:** a reflective layer in the eye enhances nocturnal hunting【999739901386276†L76-L81】.
- **Versatile locomotion:** ocelots are adept climbers and swimmers【999739901386276†L88-L95】.

## Conservation status

The ocelot is listed as **Least Concern**, but northern populations are threatened by habitat loss, road mortality and historical fur hunting.  Legal protection and habitat corridors are essential for connecting isolated populations.  The U.S. population is endangered.
