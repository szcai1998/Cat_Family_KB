# African Leopard

## Scientific classification

- **Sub‑family:** [[Pantherinae]]
- **Genus:** *Panthera*
- **Species:** *Panthera pardus*

## Physical characteristics

Leopards are medium‑sized big cats characterised by their **golden coat covered in rosettes**.  Unlike jaguar rosettes, leopard rosettes are **smaller and do not have a central spot**【103081960674531†L200-L215】.  Leopards have long bodies, relatively short legs and powerful shoulders.  Males typically weigh 60–90 kg, while females are smaller.

## Habitat and range

African leopards have one of the widest distributions of any big cat.  They inhabit sub‑Saharan Africa and parts of the Middle East, adapting to habitats ranging from tropical rainforests and savannas to mountainous and semi‑arid regions.  Leopards are highly adaptable and can live close to human settlements if prey is abundant.

## Behaviour and diet

Leopards are solitary and predominantly nocturnal.  They are expert climbers and often drag their kills into trees to protect them from scavengers such as hyenas and lions.  Their diet includes medium‑sized ungulates, monkeys, birds and occasionally fish or invertebrates.  Leopards hunt primarily by stealth, stalking to within a few metres before launching a quick pounce.

## Special traits

- **Agility and climbing ability:** leopards are built for agility and can leap several metres horizontally and vertically.  Their long tail provides balance when moving among branches.
- **Camouflage:** the rosette pattern breaks up the outline of the body in dappled light, making leopards effective ambush predators【103081960674531†L200-L215】.
- **Versatility:** leopards thrive in diverse habitats and have the broadest diet of any big cat.

## Conservation status

The African leopard is listed as **Vulnerable**.  Its adaptable nature has enabled it to persist in many areas, but threats include habitat loss, persecution by livestock owners and illegal wildlife trade.  Leopards are also often killed for their skins.
