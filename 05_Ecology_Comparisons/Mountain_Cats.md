# Mountain Cats

High‑altitude environments present unique challenges—cold temperatures, low oxygen levels, rugged terrain and sparse prey. Cats that inhabit mountains have evolved thick fur, large paws and other adaptations to survive.

## Species in this atlas

- **Snow leopard** – the quintessential mountain cat. It has a dense, greyish coat with dark rosettes, a long tail for balance and warmth, and large paws that act like snowshoes【596547831032212†L233-L265】. Snow leopards inhabit alpine and subalpine zones across Central Asia.
- **Eurasian lynx** – found in boreal forests and mountainous regions of Europe and Asia. Their thick winter coats and broad feet help them navigate snowy terrain【843266011844487†L100-L114】.
- **Pallas’s cat (manul)** – lives among rocky steppes and highland grasslands of Central Asia. Its long, dense fur and low‑set ears provide insulation and camouflage【247072692998982†L160-L173】.
- **Amur/Siberian tiger** – though primarily forest‑dwelling, Siberian tigers roam foothills and mountainous taiga where deep snow and frigid temperatures demand thick coats and large body size【362655593780840†L60-L114】.

These mountain cats illustrate how felids adapt to some of the harshest environments on Earth.
