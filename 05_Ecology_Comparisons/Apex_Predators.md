# Apex Predators

An **apex predator** sits at the top of its food web, preying on other animals but rarely falling prey itself. Apex predators regulate the abundance of herbivores and mesopredators, helping maintain the ecological balance of ecosystems. Many cats featured in this atlas are apex predators in their respective habitats.

## Apex cats in the atlas

- **Siberian/Amur tiger** – the world’s largest cat. In the boreal forests of the Russian Far East, tigers feed on deer and wild boar and have no natural predators.
- **African lion** – as pride hunters, lions dominate savanna food webs and can even displace hyenas and leopards from kills. Adult lions have no natural predators, though human conflict poses significant threats.
- **Jaguar** – the largest cat in the Americas; its powerful bite allows it to pierce turtle shells and caiman skulls.
- **Leopard** – though smaller than other Panthera cats, leopards are versatile predators that can take down animals larger than themselves and often drag kills into trees to avoid scavengers.
- **Snow leopard** – at high altitudes it preys on ibex, wild sheep and marmots and has few natural enemies.
- **Puma (cougar)** – across the Americas, pumas hunt deer and other large ungulates, playing a keystone role in regulating prey populations.

Recognising which species function as apex predators helps highlight their ecological importance and the cascading effects of their decline. Protecting apex cats often benefits entire ecosystems.
