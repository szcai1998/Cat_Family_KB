# Wetland and Grassland Cats

Wetlands and open grasslands support high densities of prey and attract cats that rely on speed and hearing. These ecosystems include African savannas, South American pampas and North American prairies.

## Species in this atlas

- **Cheetah** – the ultimate grassland sprinter, reaching speeds of 60–70 mph over short distances. Cheetahs hunt diurnally in open savannas where their keen eyesight and acceleration give them an advantage.
- **Serval** – nicknamed the “giraffe cat” for its long legs and neck, the serval hunts rodents and birds in tall grasses near wetlands.
- **Lion** – prides patrol savannas and open woodlands, preying on antelopes, zebras and buffalo.
- **Puma** – inhabits grasslands and open shrublands across the Americas; although ambush‑oriented, it can pursue prey across short stretches of open ground.
- **Jaguar** – while often associated with forests, jaguars also hunt along rivers and wetlands, feeding on fish, turtles and caimans.

These cats illustrate the diversity of hunting strategies in grassland and wetland environments—from pursuit chases to pouncing from cover.
