# Arboreal Cats

Some cats are skilled climbers, spending much of their time in trees where they hunt, rest or escape predators. Arboreal adaptations include strong forelimbs, flexible ankles and tails for balance.

## Notable arboreal species

- **Clouded leopard** – perhaps the most tree‑adapted of all cats. It has short, powerful legs and unique rotating ankles that allow it to climb down trees headfirst and even hang upside down from branches. Its long tail provides balance as it moves through the canopy.
- **Leopard** – although not strictly arboreal, leopards are excellent climbers. They often drag kills into trees to avoid scavengers.
- **Ocelot** – inhabiting dense forests, ocelots are agile climbers and swimmers that can hunt birds and small mammals in the trees.
- **Eurasian lynx** – while primarily terrestrial, lynx can climb and will perch on low branches to observe surroundings.

Other felids not covered in this atlas—such as the margay and the clouded leopard’s Sunda cousin—are even more specialized for arboreal life. These cats possess flexible ankles and long tails that help them move through complex three‑dimensional environments.
