# Desert and Dryland Cats

Drylands—including deserts, savannas and semi‑arid scrub—are home to several felids that cope with high temperatures, scarce water and open landscapes. Adaptations include long legs for speed, large ears for thermoregulation and acute hearing, and the ability to survive on minimal water.

## Species in this atlas

- **Caracal** – inhabits savannas, semi‑deserts and arid mountains across Africa and the Middle East【514282553040283†L102-L118】. Its long legs and powerful jump enable it to catch birds in flight【514282553040283†L134-L174】. Black ear tufts may aid communication.
- **Serval** – found near rivers and streams in African savannas. Although preferring wetter patches, servals are adapted to tall grasslands and are excellent hunters of rodents using their large ears to locate prey【964951246235460†L40-L74】.
- **Puma** – occupies a wide range of habitats, including deserts and dry shrublands, especially in western North America and parts of South America【671062261146697†L245-L265】. Its tawny coat blends with dry landscapes.
- **Lion** – African lions inhabit open plains, grassy savannas and dry thorn forests【831700127756655†L15-L90】. Prides often rest in the shade during the hottest parts of the day.
- **Pallas’s cat** – lives in Central Asian deserts and grasslands where temperatures can vary drastically【247072692998982†L176-L178】.

Many other small cats, such as the sand cat and black‑footed cat, specialise in deserts but are not yet part of this atlas.
