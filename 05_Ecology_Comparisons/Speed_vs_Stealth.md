# Speed vs Stealth

Cats employ different hunting strategies depending on their morphology and habitat. Two ends of this spectrum are **pursuit predators**, which rely on speed to outrun prey, and **ambush predators**, which use stealth and powerful bursts of strength to subdue prey.

## Speed specialists

- **Cheetah** – the fastest land mammal, capable of accelerating from 0 to 45 mph in 2.5 seconds. Its lightweight build, long legs and non‑retractable claws allow high‑speed chases across open terrain. Cheetahs typically stalk within 100 yards before sprinting to capture small antelope.

- **Caracal** – although not as fast as the cheetah, caracals can reach speeds up to 50 mph and are renowned for their leaping ability, using speed and agility to catch birds and small antelope.

## Stealth specialists

- **Leopard** – a master of stealth, the leopard relies on camouflage and silent stalks. Its rosette pattern blends into forest and savanna backgrounds. Leopards often ambush prey at close range and drag kills into trees to avoid scavengers.

- **Jaguar** – though capable of short bursts of speed, jaguars usually stalk and ambush prey. Their powerful bite allows them to kill large animals quickly.

- **Puma** – an ambush hunter that travels 10 km or more per night to locate prey. Pumas prefer to attack from cover and can leap several metres to pounce on deer and other ungulates.

- **Eurasian lynx** – hunts by ambush in forests and mountains, waiting silently for roe deer or smaller mammals.

Both strategies can be highly effective. Some cats, like servals and ocelots, mix stealth with sudden bursts of speed. Recognising these differences helps us understand how form and environment shape behaviour.
