# African Leopard

> **Stealthy African climbing predator**

## 1. Core Identity

- **Common name:** African leopard
- **Alternative names:** Leopard
- **Scientific name:** *Panthera pardus pardus* (regional subspecies; species *P. pardus*)
- **Taxonomic group:** Subfamily *Pantherinae*; genus *Panthera*
- **Lineage:** Leopard lineage within Panthera
- **Exhibit role:** Big cat exhibit
- **Main biome:** Savannas, woodlands and forests across Africa
- **Main hunting style:** Stealthy ambush with arboreal component

## 2. Museum Summary

The **leopard** is one of the most adaptable and widespread big cats.  Individuals weigh **66–176 pounds** (30–80 kg), with a **body length of 4.25–6.25 ft** (1.3–1.9 m) and a **tail of 3.5–4.5 ft** (1–1.4 m)【63005965708133†L25-L72】.  Leopards possess a **golden coat patterned with rosettes**—clustered spots that provide camouflage in dappled light.  Their powerful limbs and retractable claws make them excellent climbers; they often drag kills into trees to keep them safe from lions and hyenas【63005965708133†L69-L92】.  As solitary and stealthy hunters, leopards take a wide variety of prey and occupy a range of habitats from African savannas to Asian forests.  The African leopard subspecies is listed as **Vulnerable**, and populations are declining due to habitat fragmentation, poaching and persecution.

## 3. Range

- **Continents:** Africa (also parts of Asia, but this profile focuses on the African subspecies).
- **Countries/regions:** Sub‑Saharan Africa, from the Cape to the Sahel; absent from the driest deserts and tropical rainforests.
- **Range type:** Wide but fragmented; leopards persist in many protected areas and some human‑dominated landscapes.
- **Range notes:** Their adaptability allows them to coexist near rural settlements, but human conflict is increasing.

## 4. Habitat

- **Primary habitat:** Savannahs, woodlands and forest edges【63005965708133†L25-L72】.
- **Secondary habitat:** Mountains, shrublands and semi‑deserts; also occurs in rainforests where prey is abundant.
- **Climate adaptation:** Variable; coats can be lighter or darker depending on environment.
- **Terrain preference:** Prefers areas with cover—rocks, bushes or trees—to stalk prey and hide kills.

## 5. Diet

- **Main prey:** Medium‑sized ungulates such as antelopes and deer【63005965708133†L25-L72】.
- **Secondary prey:** Monkeys, pigs, dogs, birds, reptiles and occasionally fish【63005965708133†L69-L92】.
- **Hunting method:** Ambush predator; approaches within a few metres then pounces.  Dragging prey into trees to avoid scavengers is common【63005965708133†L69-L92】.
- **Feeding ecology:** Opportunistic; diets vary by habitat and season.  They are known to cache kills on tree branches.

## 6. Behaviour / Ecology

- **Social structure:** Solitary.  Adults maintain overlapping territories marked with scent.
- **Activity rhythm:** Primarily nocturnal or crepuscular, but may be active during the day in remote areas.
- **Territory:** Females occupy smaller territories; males range over larger areas overlapping several females.
- **Ecological role:** Apex or mesopredator depending on region; helps control herbivore and small mammal populations.

## 7. Build & Scale

- **Body size:** **4.25–6.25 ft** (1.3–1.9 m) long with a **tail 3.5–4.5 ft** (1–1.4 m)【63005965708133†L25-L72】.
- **Weight range:** **66–176 lb** (30–80 kg)【63005965708133†L25-L72】.
- **Key proportions:** Long body, relatively short legs, large head and strong jaws; tail aids balance when climbing.
- **Movement style:** Quiet, fluid stalk; powerful leaps; adept tree climber and swimmer【63005965708133†L69-L92】.

## 8. Signature Traits

1. **Rosette pattern:** Distinctive clusters of black spots on a golden coat provide excellent camouflage【63005965708133†L69-L92】.
2. **Arboreal habits:** Drags kills up trees to avoid hyenas and lions【63005965708133†L69-L92】.
3. **Versatility:** Inhabits a wide range of environments and consumes diverse prey【63005965708133†L25-L72】.
4. **Stealth:** Moves silently and rarely vocalises; communicates through scent marking and occasional sawing calls.

## 9. Conservation

- **IUCN status:** Vulnerable.
- **Population trend:** Decreasing; exact numbers uncertain but thought to be declining across much of Africa【63005965708133†L25-L72】.
- **Main threats:** Habitat loss, fragmentation, depletion of prey, retaliatory killings over livestock, trophy hunting and poaching for skins【63005965708133†L69-L92】.
- **Conservation notes:** Protection of habitat, reduction of human conflict, regulation of hunting and connectivity corridors are key to leopard conservation.
