# Serval

> **Long‑legged wetland/grassland listening hunter**

## 1. Core Identity

- **Common name:** Serval
- **Alternative names:** Giraffe cat
- **Scientific name:** *Leptailurus serval*
- **Taxonomic group:** Subfamily *Felinae*; genus *Leptailurus*
- **Lineage:** Caracal lineage (includes caracal, serval and African golden cat)
- **Exhibit role:** Grassland/wetland cat exhibit
- **Main biome:** African savannas and wetlands
- **Main hunting style:** Sit‑and‑listen hunter with explosive leaps

## 2. Museum Summary

The **serval** is a slender African cat recognised by its **extraordinarily long legs** and **oversized ears**.  Adults measure **73–100 cm** in body length with a **tail 26–41 cm**, and weigh **7–15.5 kg** (males) or **6.6–11.3 kg** (females)【655136759873302†L175-L183】.  The serval’s small head, long neck and long legs give it a “giraffe‑like” profile【964951246235460†L40-L74】.  It inhabits wet savannas and grasslands near rivers and marshes, using its acute hearing to locate rodents and frogs hidden in tall grass【964951246235460†L40-L74】.  Servals can leap up to **9 ft** (2.7 m) straight into the air to pounce on prey【964951246235460†L40-L74】.  They are classified as **Least Concern**, though the North African subspecies is endangered【964951246235460†L56-L79】.

## 3. Range

- **Continents:** Africa.
- **Countries/regions:** Widespread in sub‑Saharan Africa except for dense rainforests and desert regions【964951246235460†L56-L79】.
- **Range type:** Extensive but patchy; absent from North Africa except for remnant subspecies (critically endangered).
- **Range notes:** Prefers areas with tall grasses and wetlands; absent from very open or densely forested environments.

## 4. Habitat

- **Primary habitat:** Wet savannas, reed beds and marshes near rivers and streams【964951246235460†L40-L74】.
- **Secondary habitat:** Grasslands, lightly wooded savannas and agricultural fields; avoids true desert and dense forest【964951246235460†L56-L79】.
- **Climate adaptation:** Long legs allow servals to see above tall grasses and wade through water; large ears dissipate heat and detect sounds of prey.
- **Terrain preference:** Prefers areas with tall vegetation for cover and open patches for stalking and pouncing.

## 5. Diet

- **Main prey:** Small rodents such as rats and mice【964951246235460†L40-L74】.
- **Secondary prey:** Birds, reptiles, frogs, insects, crabs and occasionally fish【964951246235460†L40-L74】.
- **Hunting method:** Uses acute hearing to detect prey; stalks quietly then leaps high to pounce down with front paws outstretched【964951246235460†L40-L74】.
- **Feeding ecology:** Servals kill multiple small prey throughout the night; may store surplus in vegetation or eat on the spot.

## 6. Behaviour / Ecology

- **Social structure:** Solitary; males and females come together only to mate; females raise 1–3 kittens alone【964951246235460†L56-L79】.
- **Activity rhythm:** Mostly nocturnal or crepuscular; may hunt during the day in undisturbed areas【964951246235460†L56-L79】.
- **Territory:** Home ranges vary between 10 and 20 km²; males have larger territories overlapping several females.
- **Ecological role:** Controls populations of rodents and small vertebrates; prey for larger predators such as leopards and hyenas.

## 7. Build & Scale

- **Body size:** **73–100 cm** body length; **tail 26–41 cm**【655136759873302†L175-L183】.
- **Weight range:** **6.6–11.3 kg** (females); **7–15.5 kg** (males)【655136759873302†L175-L183】.
- **Key proportions:** Longest legs relative to body size among cats; large oval ears; slim body and small head【964951246235460†L40-L74】.
- **Movement style:** Walks gracefully through tall grass; leaps high from standing start; agile runner but not built for sustained speed.

## 8. Signature Traits

1. **Long legs and neck:** Facilitate seeing over tall grasses and jumping high【964951246235460†L40-L74】.
2. **Oversized ears:** Provide exceptional hearing to detect underground or hidden prey【964951246235460†L40-L74】.
3. **Black spotted coat:** Golden to tawny background with bold black spots and bands.
4. **Pouncing ability:** Can spring straight up to snatch birds mid‑air【964951246235460†L40-L74】.

## 9. Conservation

- **IUCN status:** Least Concern overall; North African subspecies is endangered【964951246235460†L56-L79】.
- **Population trend:** Stable in many areas but declining where wetlands are drained or converted to agriculture.
- **Main threats:** Habitat loss (wetland drainage), persecution for poultry predation, hunting for pelts, capture for the exotic pet trade and road mortality【964951246235460†L56-L79】.
- **Conservation notes:** Protected in many countries; maintaining wetlands and promoting coexistence with farmers are priorities.  Captive breeding is rare due to wide distribution in the wild.
