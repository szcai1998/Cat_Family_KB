# Andean Mountain Cat

> The sacred ghost of the high peaks, wrapped in a luxuriant silver coat above the clouds.

## 1. Core Identity

- **Common name:** Andean Mountain Cat
- **Alternative names:** Andean Cat, Sacred Cat of the Andes
- **Scientific name:** _Leopardus jacobita_
- **Taxonomic group:** Felinae, Leopardus
- **Lineage:** Ocelot lineage
- **Exhibit role:** The High-Altitude Specialist
- **Main biome:** High Andes mountains (above tree-line)
- **Main hunting style:** Rocky terrain ambush

## 2. Museum Summary

The Andean mountain cat is one of the rarest and most elusive felines on Earth. Dwelling exclusively above the tree-line in the freezing, barren peaks of the Andes—often between 3,000 and 4,500 meters—it survives where the air is desperately thin and the temperatures plunge far below zero. To combat this extreme environment, it is wrapped in an incredibly dense, silvery-grey coat and sports a massive, thick tail that it wraps around itself like a blanket when sleeping. For centuries, it has been revered as a sacred spirit by indigenous Andean cultures, a silent watcher of the high mountains.

## 3. Range

- **Continents:** South America
- **Countries/regions:** Argentina, Bolivia, Chile, and Peru.
- **Range type:** Extremely fragmented and highly localized.
- **Range notes:** Populations are isolated on specific mountain peaks, acting like "islands in the sky."

## 4. Habitat

- **Primary habitat:** Arid, rocky terrain above the tree-line (Puna and altiplano ecosystems).
- **Secondary habitat:** High-elevation steppe.
- **Climate adaptation:** Possesses incredibly thick, insulating fur and a specialized circulatory system to deal with hypoxia (low oxygen).
- **Terrain preference:** Requires steep, rocky cliffs and boulders for hunting and denning.

## 5. Diet

- **Main prey:** Mountain vizcachas (a large, rabbit-like rodent) and chinchillas.
- **Secondary prey:** Small birds, reptiles, and smaller rodents.
- **Hunting method:** Terrestrial stalk-and-pounce; uses the complex rocky terrain for ambush.
- **Feeding ecology:** Its survival is almost entirely dependent on the presence of vizcacha colonies.

## 6. Behaviour / Ecology

- **Social structure:** Solitary.
- **Activity rhythm:** Primarily diurnal (active during the day) to match the activity of its main prey, the vizcacha.
- **Territory:** Enormous home ranges relative to its size, as prey colonies are sparse and widely separated.
- **Ecological role:** Apex predator of the high-altitude rocky steppe.

## 7. Build & Scale

- **Body size:** 57–64 cm (22–25 in) body, with a tail up to 48 cm (19 in).
- **Weight range:** 4–6 kg (9–13 lbs).
- **Key proportions:** Looks much larger than it actually is due to its extremely thick fur. Its tail is disproportionately long and thick.
- **Movement style:** Agile leaping across boulders and steep scree slopes.

## 8. Signature Traits

- **Thermal-Blanket Tail:** The tail is massive, accounting for nearly 70% of its body length, and is covered in long, dense fur. It is actively wrapped around the cat's face and body when sleeping to prevent heat loss.
- **Specialized Auditory Bullae:** The hollow, bony structures enclosing the middle ear are enlarged, enhancing its hearing to detect the faint scuffles of vizcachas in the vast, echoing mountain valleys.
- **Sacred Status:** The cat is heavily featured in Andean mythology as a symbol of fertility and water. Tragically, this has led to hunting, as their stuffed pelts are sometimes used in traditional ceremonies.

## 9. Conservation

- **IUCN status:** Endangered (EN)
- **Population trend:** Decreasing (estimated fewer than 1,400 mature individuals remain).
- **Main threats:** Habitat degradation by mining and unregulated tourism, hunting for ceremonial purposes, and the decline of its primary prey (chinchillas).
- **Conservation notes:** The Andean Cat Alliance (AGA) is working across four countries to protect this species, primarily by collaborating with local communities to end ceremonial hunting.

- **Preview Card:** An Andean mountain cat wrapped in its thick tail, sleeping peacefully on a snow-dusted, high-altitude boulder.
