# Jaguar

> **Largest cat of the Americas**

## 1. Core Identity

- **Common name:** Jaguar
- **Alternative names:** El tigre (in Spanish), painted onça (in Portuguese)
- **Scientific name:** *Panthera onca*
- **Taxonomic group:** Subfamily *Pantherinae*; genus *Panthera*
- **Lineage:** Jaguar lineage (within Panthera)
- **Exhibit role:** Big cat exhibit
- **Main biome:** Tropical rainforests, wetlands and dry forests
- **Main hunting style:** Ambush predator with exceptionally strong bite

## 2. Museum Summary

The **jaguar** is the largest cat of the Americas and the third largest cat species worldwide.  Adults measure **5–6 ft** (1.5–1.8 m) in body length with a **tail of 27.5–36 in** (70–90 cm) and weigh **100–250 pounds** (45–113 kg)【174786318036718†L14-L97】.  Their **tawny coat is adorned with dark rosettes** that enclose central spots, creating a striking pattern.  Jaguars are powerful swimmers and climbers; their massive skull and jaw muscles produce a bite strong enough to pierce turtle shells or the skulls of caimans【174786318036718†L14-L97】.  Historically ranged from the southwestern United States to Argentina, they are now mainly confined to the Amazon Basin.  Jaguars are listed as **Near Threatened** due to habitat loss, fragmentation and poaching【174786318036718†L100-L132】.

## 3. Range

- **Continents:** South America and Central America; formerly North America.
- **Countries/regions:** Dense populations in the Amazon rainforests of Brazil, Peru and Colombia; remnant populations in Central America and Mexico; extirpated from much of the United States【174786318036718†L14-L97】.
- **Range type:** Fragmented; populations isolated by deforestation and human development【174786318036718†L100-L132】.
- **Range notes:** Conservation corridors aim to reconnect jaguar habitats across Central and South America.

## 4. Habitat

- **Primary habitat:** Tropical rainforests and wetlands with dense cover and proximity to rivers【174786318036718†L14-L97】.
- **Secondary habitat:** Dry forests, savannas and scrublands, especially where prey is abundant.
- **Climate adaptation:** Thick coat provides camouflage in shadowed forests; ability to swim across rivers and flooded forests.
- **Terrain preference:** Prefers areas with water; often found near rivers, swamps and lagoons.

## 5. Diet

- **Main prey:** Medium‑ to large‑sized mammals such as deer, peccaries, capybaras and tapirs【174786318036718†L14-L97】.
- **Secondary prey:** Fish, turtles, caimans, birds and smaller mammals【174786318036718†L14-L97】.
- **Hunting method:** Ambush predator; stalks prey silently and delivers a crushing bite, often to the skull.  Jaguars are strong swimmers and may hunt aquatic prey【174786318036718†L14-L97】.
- **Feeding ecology:** Solitary feeders; drag kills to secluded spots; may bury large kills and return later.

## 6. Behaviour / Ecology

- **Social structure:** Solitary except during mating or when females raise cubs.  Cubs stay with their mother for up to two years【174786318036718†L14-L97】.
- **Activity rhythm:** Mostly nocturnal or crepuscular; may be active during the day in remote areas.
- **Territory:** Home range size varies with prey abundance; males overlap with several females but exclude other males.
- **Ecological role:** Apex predator controlling populations of herbivores and mesopredators; important indicator of forest health.

## 7. Build & Scale

- **Body size:** **5–6 ft** body length; **27.5–36 in** tail【174786318036718†L14-L97】.
- **Weight range:** **100–250 lb** (45–113 kg)【174786318036718†L14-L97】.
- **Key proportions:** Stocky and muscular with a large head and short limbs; paws and claws powerful for grasping prey.
- **Movement style:** Stalks quietly; excellent at swimming and climbing; can leap onto prey with explosive force.

## 8. Signature Traits

1. **Rosette pattern with central spots:** Distinguishes jaguars from leopards, whose rosettes lack central dots【174786318036718†L14-L97】.
2. **Massive bite force:** Strongest bite relative to body size among cats, enabling jaguars to penetrate armour‑like turtle shells【174786318036718†L14-L97】.
3. **Aquatic prowess:** Adept swimmer that hunts fish, turtles and caimans【174786318036718†L14-L97】.
4. **Territorial vocalisations:** Jaguars produce deep roars and grunts to communicate and advertise territory.

## 9. Conservation

- **IUCN status:** Near Threatened.
- **Population trend:** Decreasing; primary stronghold in Amazon Basin; outside Amazon numbers are low【174786318036718†L100-L132】.
- **Main threats:** Habitat loss from deforestation, illegal hunting of jaguars and their prey, retaliatory killing by ranchers, and poaching for the illegal trade【174786318036718†L100-L132】.
- **Conservation notes:** Initiatives focus on creating habitat corridors, reducing livestock conflict and enforcing anti‑poaching laws.  Indigenous community involvement is crucial.
