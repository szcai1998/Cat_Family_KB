# Oncilla

> The spotted ghost of the scrublands, navigating the thorny underbrush with delicate precision.

## 1. Core Identity

- **Common name:** Oncilla
- **Alternative names:** Northern Tiger Cat, little spotted cat, tigrillo
- **Scientific name:** _Leopardus tigrinus_
- **Taxonomic group:** Felinae, Leopardus
- **Lineage:** Ocelot lineage
- **Exhibit role:** The Spotted Ghost of the Scrub
- **Main biome:** Savanna and shrubland (Cerrado/Caatinga)
- **Main hunting style:** Stalk-and-pounce

## 2. Museum Summary

Barely larger than a domestic kitten, the oncilla—or Northern Tiger Cat—is a fragile beauty traversing the harsh, semi-arid scrublands of South America. It is the smallest of the spotted Neotropical cats, often confused with its larger cousins, the ocelot and margay. Unlike the canopy-dwelling margay, the oncilla keeps its paws firmly on the ground, threading through the dense, thorny bushes of the Brazilian Caatinga and Cerrado. It is a master of invisibility, relying on its disruptive camouflage to evade both larger predators and the watchful eyes of its tiny prey.

## 3. Range

- **Continents:** Central America, South America
- **Countries/regions:** Costa Rica down into northern South America, extending heavily into northern and central Brazil.
- **Range type:** Highly fragmented.
- **Range notes:** Recent genetic studies suggest the southern populations might actually be a completely distinct species (the Southern Tiger Cat, _Leopardus guttulus_).

## 4. Habitat

- **Primary habitat:** Semi-arid scrub (Caatinga), tropical savannas (Cerrado), and subtropical forests.
- **Secondary habitat:** Cloud forests and eucalyptus plantations.
- **Climate adaptation:** Capable of surviving in drier, harsher climates than most other spotted forest cats.
- **Terrain preference:** Dense understory cover for hunting and hiding.

## 5. Diet

- **Main prey:** Small terrestrial rodents and shrews.
- **Secondary prey:** Small birds, lizards, and insects.
- **Hunting method:** Terrestrial stalk-and-pounce.
- **Feeding ecology:** Highly opportunistic, hunting whatever small mammals are locally abundant in the scrub.

## 6. Behaviour / Ecology

- **Social structure:** Strictly solitary.
- **Activity rhythm:** Nocturnal (night-active) and crepuscular (twilight-active).
- **Territory:** Large territories relative to its tiny size to find enough prey in arid environments.
- **Ecological role:** Micro-predator controlling rodent populations.

## 7. Build & Scale

- **Body size:** 38–59 cm (15–23 in).
- **Weight range:** 1.5–3.5 kg (3.3–8 lbs).
- **Key proportions:** Very slender build, narrow head, and large, rounded ears.
- **Movement style:** Delicate, quiet stalking through dry leaves and thorny brush.

## 8. Signature Traits

- **Micro-Scale Camouflage:** Its spots are smaller and more densely packed than a margay's, creating "disruptive coloration" that breaks up its outline in dappled scrublight.
- **Melanism:** Up to 20% of some oncilla populations exhibit melanism (all-black coats), helping them camouflage in dense, dark forests.
- **Extreme Agility:** While not strictly arboreal, they are excellent climbers when escaping predators.

## 9. Conservation

- **IUCN status:** Vulnerable (VU)
- **Population trend:** Decreasing.
- **Main threats:** Habitat conversion to soy and cattle farming, and historically, intense hunting for its beautiful pelt.
- **Conservation notes:** Protected in Brazil, but enforcement across the vast, remote Cerrado is extremely difficult.
