# Caracal

> **Black‑eared dryland jumper/hunter**

## 1. Core Identity

- **Common name:** Caracal
- **Alternative names:** Desert lynx (misleading; caracal is not a lynx)
- **Scientific name:** *Caracal caracal*
- **Taxonomic group:** Subfamily *Felinae*; genus *Caracal*
- **Lineage:** Caracal lineage
- **Exhibit role:** Dryland cat exhibit
- **Main biome:** Savannas, semi‑deserts and arid mountains
- **Main hunting style:** Ambush and pounce; impressive jumping ability

## 2. Museum Summary

The **caracal** is a medium‑sized cat distinguished by its **long, black ear tufts** and sleek, tawny coat.  Adults measure up to **1 m** (3.3 ft) in length and weigh **8–18 kg** (18–40 lb)【514282553040283†L102-L118】.  They can sprint at **50 mph** (80 km/h) and leap up to **3–4 m** vertically to snatch birds in flight【514282553040283†L131-L159】.  Caracals inhabit a wide range across Africa and the Middle East to north‑western India, thriving in savannas, scrub, deserts and arid mountains【514282553040283†L102-L118】.  Solitary by nature, they prey on rodents, hares, hyraxes and small antelope, and are known for taking prey much larger than themselves【514282553040283†L131-L159】.  Caracals are classified as **Least Concern**, though they face localised threats from habitat loss and persecution.

## 3. Range

- **Continents:** Africa and Asia.
- **Countries/regions:** Sub‑Saharan Africa, North Africa, Arabian Peninsula, Iran, Afghanistan, Pakistan and north‑west India【514282553040283†L102-L118】.
- **Range type:** Broad and continuous across much of Africa; fragmented in Asia.
- **Range notes:** Absent from true rainforests and extreme deserts.  Occasionally inhabits highland plateaus.

## 4. Habitat

- **Primary habitat:** Savannas, dry woodlands and semi‑deserts【514282553040283†L102-L118】.
- **Secondary habitat:** Rocky hills and arid mountains; can live near agriculture if prey is available【514282553040283†L102-L118】.
- **Climate adaptation:** Thick fur on ear tufts may protect against sand and sun; capable of surviving long periods without water.
- **Terrain preference:** Prefers areas with cover such as bushes or tall grass for stalking; avoids dense rainforest.

## 5. Diet

- **Main prey:** Rodents, hares and hyraxes【514282553040283†L131-L159】.
- **Secondary prey:** Birds (can leap to catch in flight), small antelopes and occasionally livestock and larger prey【514282553040283†L131-L159】.
- **Hunting method:** Ambushes and pounces; uses powerful hind legs to leap high or chase down prey.  Stealthy stalker that can stand on hind legs to scan for birds.
- **Feeding ecology:** Usually consumes entire small prey and caches leftovers.  May drink infrequently, obtaining moisture from prey.

## 6. Behaviour / Ecology

- **Social structure:** Solitary except for mating and mother–young pairs【514282553040283†L120-L124】.
- **Activity rhythm:** Primarily nocturnal or crepuscular; may be diurnal in undisturbed habitats【514282553040283†L120-L124】.
- **Territory:** Males occupy larger territories overlapping those of several females; females are more sedentary.
- **Ecological role:** Controls populations of rodents and small ungulates; prey for larger carnivores like lions and hyenas【514282553040283†L176-L183】.

## 7. Build & Scale

- **Body size:** Length up to **1 m** (3.3 ft) excluding tail【514282553040283†L102-L118】; tail adds 20–34 cm【40683461462749†L136-L145】.
- **Weight range:** **8–18 kg** (18–40 lb)【514282553040283†L102-L118】.
- **Key proportions:** Long legs, slender body, short tail; large pointed ears with black tufts up to 5 cm【40683461462749†L40-L69】.
- **Movement style:** Agile sprinter with explosive jump; runs at speeds up to **50 mph**【514282553040283†L131-L159】.

## 8. Signature Traits

1. **Black ear tufts:** Used for communication and camouflage【40683461462749†L40-L69】.
2. **Impressive jump:** Leaps vertically up to 3–4 m to catch birds mid‑air【514282553040283†L131-L159】.
3. **Tufted tail tip:** Tail tip is black, contrasting with tawny body.
4. **Adaptability:** Occupies diverse dryland habitats across Africa and southwest Asia【514282553040283†L102-L118】.

## 9. Conservation

- **IUCN status:** Least Concern.
- **Population trend:** Stable overall but decreasing in parts of North Africa and Asia【514282553040283†L176-L183】.
- **Main threats:** Habitat loss, persecution for poultry and livestock predation, hunting for pelts, road mortality and hybridisation with domestic cats in some regions.
- **Conservation notes:** Listed in CITES Appendix II; protected in many countries.  Education and livestock protection measures reduce conflict.
