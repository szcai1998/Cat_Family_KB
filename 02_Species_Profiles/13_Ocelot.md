# Ocelot

> **Neotropical spotted forest hunter**

## 1. Core Identity

- **Common name:** Ocelot
- **Alternative names:** Painted leopard, dwarf leopard
- **Scientific name:** *Leopardus pardalis*
- **Taxonomic group:** Subfamily *Felinae*; genus *Leopardus*
- **Lineage:** Ocelot lineage (includes margay and oncilla)
- **Exhibit role:** Neotropical cat exhibit
- **Main biome:** Tropical and subtropical forests, dense chaparral
- **Main hunting style:** Stealthy stalking and ambush

## 2. Museum Summary

The **ocelot** is a medium‑sized spotted cat inhabiting the forests and scrublands of the Americas.  Adults measure **73–100 cm** in body length with a **tail 26–41 cm** and weigh **7–15.5 kg** (males) or **6.6–11.3 kg** (females)【655136759873302†L175-L183】.  Their **coat is golden to reddish‑brown with distinct black and dark brown rosettes and stripes**, including stripes on the cheeks and a ringed tail【655136759873302†L50-L63】.  Ocelots are nocturnal, secretive and excellent climbers and swimmers【655136759873302†L94-L115】.  They hunt mainly small mammals and birds under cover of dense vegetation but will also take reptiles, fish and crabs【655136759873302†L94-L115】.  Ocelots range from South Texas through Central America to northern Argentina.  While the species is listed as **Least Concern**, the U.S. subspecies is **Endangered** with fewer than 100 individuals【655136759873302†L129-L136】.

## 3. Range

- **Continents:** North America and South America.
- **Countries/regions:** Southern Texas (USA), Mexico, Central America, northern South America down to northern Argentina【655136759873302†L50-L63】.
- **Range type:** Broad in Central and South America; fragmented and critically small in the United States【655136759873302†L129-L136】.
- **Range notes:** Prefers areas with dense cover; range is limited by deforestation and habitat conversion.

## 4. Habitat

- **Primary habitat:** Tropical rainforests, mangroves and gallery forests【655136759873302†L50-L63】.
- **Secondary habitat:** Dense chaparral, thorn scrub and semi‑deciduous forests【655136759873302†L50-L63】.
- **Climate adaptation:** Adaptable to warm, humid climates; frequently found near water.
- **Terrain preference:** Dense vegetation and forest edges; avoids open grasslands and human settlements.

## 5. Diet

- **Main prey:** Small mammals (rodents), especially during the night【655136759873302†L94-L115】.
- **Secondary prey:** Birds, snakes, iguanas, young deer, rabbits, fish and crabs【655136759873302†L94-L115】.
- **Hunting method:** Stealthy stalker; hunts by scent and sound; may cover unused portions of a kill and return later【655136759873302†L94-L115】.
- **Feeding ecology:** Opportunistic feeder; diet varies by habitat and season; strong climber and swimmer.

## 6. Behaviour / Ecology

- **Social structure:** Solitary; adults only meet to mate; females raise 1–4 kittens alone【655136759873302†L76-L92】.
- **Activity rhythm:** Nocturnal; spends daytime resting in trees or dense vegetation【655136759873302†L76-L92】.
- **Territory:** Home ranges vary from 2–30 km²; males ranges larger than females.
- **Ecological role:** Mesopredator controlling rodent and bird populations; prey for larger carnivores like jaguars and pumas.

## 7. Build & Scale

- **Body size:** **73–100 cm** body length; **tail 26–41 cm**【655136759873302†L175-L183】.
- **Weight range:** **6.6–11.3 kg** (females); **7–15.5 kg** (males)【655136759873302†L175-L183】.
- **Key proportions:** Slender body, relatively long tail, large rounded ears, striking coat pattern.
- **Movement style:** Agile climber and swimmer; moves silently through dense vegetation.

## 8. Signature Traits

1. **Intricate coat pattern:** Complex rosettes and stripes provide camouflage in dappled forest light【655136759873302†L50-L63】.
2. **Nocturnal habits:** Active at night; excellent low‑light vision【655136759873302†L76-L92】.
3. **Versatile hunter:** Able to swim and climb to catch fish and arboreal prey【655136759873302†L94-L115】.
4. **Endangered subspecies:** The Texas subspecies is critically endangered with fewer than 100 individuals【655136759873302†L129-L136】.

## 9. Conservation

- **IUCN status:** Least Concern (species); U.S. subspecies Endangered【655136759873302†L129-L136】.
- **Population trend:** Stable across most of its range; declining in parts of Central America and the United States.
- **Main threats:** Habitat loss and fragmentation due to deforestation, road mortality, illegal pet trade and historical fur trapping【655136759873302†L94-L115】.
- **Conservation notes:** Conservation in the U.S. focuses on habitat connectivity and reducing road kills.  In Latin America, protected areas and sustainable forest management are key.
