# Cheetah

> **Speed specialist grassland hunter**

## 1. Core Identity

- **Common name:** Cheetah
- **Alternative names:** None (the species is widely known simply as the cheetah)
- **Scientific name:** *Acinonyx jubatus*
- **Taxonomic group:** Subfamily *Felinae*; genus *Acinonyx*
- **Lineage:** Puma lineage
- **Exhibit role:** Speed specialist exhibit
- **Main biome:** Open savannas and grasslands
- **Main hunting style:** Pursuit predator using extreme speed

## 2. Museum Summary

The **cheetah** is the fastest land mammal, built for sheer acceleration and top speed.  Its slender body, deep chest and long limbs allow it to reach **60–70 mph** (97–113 km/h) over short distances【714516920898633†L170-L210】.  Cheetahs can accelerate from 0 to 45 mph (72 km/h) in just **2.5 seconds**, faster than many race cars【714516920898633†L170-L210】.  Adults weigh **75–140 pounds** (34–64 kg) and stand about **30 inches** (76 cm) at the shoulder【714516920898633†L223-L242】.  Their coat is sandy yellow with solid black spots, and distinctive dark “tear marks” run from the eyes to the mouth.  Semi‑retractable claws and specialised paw pads provide traction during high‑speed chases【714516920898633†L170-L210】.  Cheetahs stalk and sprint after small antelope on African savannas and are listed as **Vulnerable**.

## 3. Range

- **Continents:** Africa (with a critically endangered population in Iran).
- **Countries/regions:** Scattered across sub‑Saharan Africa; strongholds in Namibia, Botswana and Tanzania; small numbers in North Africa and Iran【714516920898633†L223-L242】.
- **Range type:** Patchy and declining; population fragmented by habitat loss and human expansion.
- **Range notes:** Once widespread across Africa, the Middle East and India; now extirpated from most of its historic range.

## 4. Habitat

- **Primary habitat:** Open savannas, grasslands and semi‑deserts【714516920898633†L223-L242】.
- **Secondary habitat:** Sparse bush and dry forest edges; avoids dense vegetation where speed cannot be used effectively.
- **Climate adaptation:** Short fur and large nasal passages facilitate cooling and oxygen intake during sprints.
- **Terrain preference:** Prefers flat or gently rolling terrain with good visibility for stalking prey.

## 5. Diet

- **Main prey:** Small to medium antelopes such as gazelles and impalas【714516920898633†L170-L210】.
- **Secondary prey:** Hares, young wildebeest, and the young of larger ungulates【714516920898633†L170-L210】.
- **Hunting method:** Stalks to within 30–100 m of prey then explodes into a sprint; uses front legs to trip prey and suffocates by biting the throat.
- **Feeding ecology:** Feeds quickly to avoid kleptoparasites like lions, hyenas and vultures; may lose kills to stronger predators.

## 6. Behaviour / Ecology

- **Social structure:** Solitary for females; males may form **coalitions** (usually brothers) that cooperate to defend territories and hunt.【Note】 (behaviour described in literature; not in cited sources, but widely documented).
- **Activity rhythm:** Diurnal, with peaks in early morning and late afternoon to avoid heat and competition【714516920898633†L170-L210】.
- **Territory:** Females have large home ranges following prey movements; male coalitions defend smaller territories rich in prey and females.
- **Ecological role:** Mesopredator; influences populations of small antelope and provides carrion for scavengers.

## 7. Build & Scale

- **Body size:** Body length **44–56 in** (112–142 cm) with a **tail 26–33 in** (66–84 cm)【714516920898633†L223-L242】.
- **Weight range:** **75–140 lb** (34–64 kg)【714516920898633†L223-L242】.
- **Key proportions:** Long legs, deep chest, lightweight frame; small rounded head with high‑set eyes; non‑retractable or semi‑retractable claws【714516920898633†L170-L210】.
- **Movement style:** Built for acceleration and speed rather than endurance; can maintain top speed for 200–300 m.【714516920898633†L170-L210】.

## 8. Signature Traits

1. **Tear marks:** Black stripes from eyes to mouth reduce sun glare and aid focus during chases【714516920898633†L170-L210】.
2. **Semi‑retractable claws and rough paw pads:** Provide traction like cleats, essential for rapid turns【714516920898633†L170-L210】.
3. **Flexible spine:** Acts like a spring, lengthening stride during high‑speed runs.
4. **Diurnal hunting:** Unusual among big cats; reduces competition and conflict with nocturnal predators【714516920898633†L170-L210】.

## 9. Conservation

- **IUCN status:** Vulnerable.
- **Population trend:** Declining; fewer than 7,000 adult cheetahs remain in the wild (estimate outside citation).
- **Main threats:** Habitat loss and fragmentation, human–wildlife conflict, decline of prey populations, illegal pet trade, and genetic bottlenecks.
- **Conservation notes:** Programmes include livestock guard dogs, community outreach, habitat connectivity and efforts to reduce illegal trade.
