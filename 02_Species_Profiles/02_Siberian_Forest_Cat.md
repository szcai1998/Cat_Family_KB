# Siberian Forest Cat

> **Natural domestic cold‑region cat**

## 1. Core Identity

- **Common name:** Siberian forest cat
- **Alternative names:** Siberian cat, Moscow longhair
- **Scientific name:** *Felis catus* (domestic breed)
- **Taxonomic group:** Subfamily *Felinae*; species *Felis catus*
- **Lineage:** Domestic cat lineage
- **Exhibit role:** Domestic/natural breed profile
- **Main biome:** Human households; historically cold northern forests of Russia
- **Main hunting style:** Rodent control (domestic) and playful pouncing

## 2. Museum Summary

The **Siberian forest cat** is a natural landrace originating in Russia’s cold northern forests.  Unlike the wild species in this atlas, it is a domestic breed of **medium‑to‑large size** with a **stocky, barrel‑shaped body** and **sturdy legs**【828266007247109†L134-L170】.  Adult males typically weigh **15–20 pounds** (6.8–9 kg) and females slightly less【828266007247109†L134-L170】.  Its defining feature is a **semi‑long triple coat**—consisting of guard hairs, awn hairs and a soft downy undercoat—that evolved to withstand Siberia’s brutal winters【828266007247109†L134-L170】.  This breed is affectionate, curious and energetic, often described as “dog‑like” in its loyalty.  Many people also consider it hypoallergenic because it may produce lower levels of the Fel d 1 allergen, although results vary between individuals.

## 3. Range

- **Continents:** Originally Asia (Europe and Asia); now worldwide as a domestic breed.
- **Countries/regions:** Native to Russia; exported globally.
- **Range type:** Domesticated; breed distributed through cat fancy and pet ownership.
- **Range notes:** Not a wild cat; originated in Russian forests and farms where it controlled rodents.

## 4. Habitat

- **Primary habitat:** Domestic homes.  Historically associated with Russian farms and villages.
- **Secondary habitat:** None (fully domesticated).  Ancestors lived in cold forests.
- **Climate adaptation:** Triple coat thickens in winter and sheds in summer【828266007247109†L134-L170】.
- **Terrain preference:** Adaptable to indoor and outdoor environments; enjoys climbing and exploring.

## 5. Diet

- **Main prey:** None in domestic context (commercial cat food).  Historically hunted mice, rats and other small rodents.
- **Secondary prey:** Birds and insects when outdoors.
- **Hunting method:** Pounce and play; domestic cats retain instinctual hunting behaviours.
- **Feeding ecology:** Opportunistic feeders; require balanced diet provided by owners.

## 6. Behaviour / Ecology

- **Social structure:** Friendly and outgoing; bonds strongly with humans; can get along with other pets.
- **Activity rhythm:** Flexible; active when engaged by owners; may be more playful in the evening.
- **Territory:** Not territorial like wild cats but may prefer certain spaces in the home.
- **Ecological role:** Historically controlled rodent populations on Russian farms; now primarily companion animals.

## 7. Build & Scale

- **Body size:** Medium to large with a muscular build and rounded abdomen【828266007247109†L134-L170】.
- **Weight range:** **15–20 pounds** (males); **10–15 pounds** (females)【828266007247109†L134-L170】.
- **Key proportions:** Broad chest, short neck, strong legs and large rounded paws; head is broad with large oval eyes.
- **Movement style:** Agile climber and jumper; graceful but heavier than many domestic breeds.

## 8. Signature Traits

1. **Triple‑layered coat:** Guard hairs repel water; awn hairs insulate; downy undercoat provides warmth【828266007247109†L134-L170】.
2. **Barrel‑shaped body:** Contributes to sturdy appearance and ability to withstand cold【828266007247109†L134-L170】.
3. **Friendly temperament:** Social, curious and playful; enjoys interactive play with humans.
4. **Possible hypoallergenic qualities:** Some individuals may produce lower levels of Fel d 1 allergen (anecdotal).

## 9. Conservation

- **IUCN status:** Not applicable – domestic species.
- **Population trend:** Popularity increasing as a recognised breed.
- **Main threats:** None in the wild; responsible breeding is important to maintain health and genetic diversity.
- **Conservation notes:** Breed registries maintain standards and monitor genetic health; no wild conservation concerns.
