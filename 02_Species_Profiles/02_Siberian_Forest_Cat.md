# Siberian Forest Cat

> **A rugged Russian treasure, wrapped in a plush triple coat designed to brave the sub-zero taiga**

## 1. Core Identity

- **Common name:** Siberian forest cat
- **Alternative names:** Siberian cat, Moscow longhair
- **Scientific name:** _Felis catus_ (domestic breed)
- **Taxonomic group:** Subfamily _Felinae_; species _Felis catus_
- **Lineage:** Domestic cat lineage
- **Exhibit role:** Domestic/natural breed profile
- **Main biome:** Human households; historically cold northern forests of Russia
- **Main hunting style:** Rodent control (domestic) and playful pouncing

## 2. Museum Summary

Emerging from the frosted wilderness of northern Russia, the **Siberian forest cat** is a testament to nature's ability to sculpt domestic animals. It is a natural landrace (a local domestic variety that developed naturally over time through adaptation rather than selective breeding), having spent centuries as a farm cat and household guardian in the bitter cold of Siberia. With a barrel-shaped, muscular body and sturdy limbs, this robust feline looks ready to leap out of a forest clearing, carrying a wild nobility that sets it apart from more delicate domestic breeds.

To survive the sub-zero winters of its homeland, the Siberian forest cat evolved a remarkable, water-resistant triple coat. This plush layer includes a dense, soft undercoat for trapping heat, a sturdy middle coat, and a weather-hardened outer layer of guard hairs that repels snow and rain. Despite its wild history and formidable appearance, this cat displays a remarkably gentle, dog-like devotion to humans. It is known for its playful intelligence, high leaping ability, and its status as a partially hypoallergenic companion—meaning it may produce less Fel d 1 (the specific protein in saliva that triggers cat allergies), allowing many allergy sufferers to enjoy its company.

## 3. Range

- **Continents:** Originally Asia (Europe and Asia); now worldwide as a popular domestic breed.
- **Countries/regions:** Native to Russia; exported globally.
- **Range type:** Domesticated; breed distributed through cat fancy registries and pet ownership.
- **Range notes:** Unlike the wild species in this atlas, the Siberian cat is domestic, tracing its origins to the farms, monasteries, and markets of northern Russia.

## 4. Habitat

- **Primary habitat:** Domestic homes and urban environments.
- **Secondary habitat:** Russian farms and rural villages, where its ancestors kept granaries safe.
- **Climate adaptation:** Triple-layered coat thickens significantly in winter—forming a heavy ruff around the neck and throat—and sheds in the summer.
- **Terrain preference:** Highly adaptable; retains an instinctive love for climbing to high vantages and exploring outdoor enclosures.

## 5. Diet

- **Main prey:** None in a domestic context (primarily commercial cat food). Historically, it hunted mice, rats, and other small rodents.
- **Secondary prey:** Wild birds and small insects when allowed outdoors.
- **Hunting method:** Playful stalk-and-pounce; domestic cats retain all the predatory motor patterns of their wild ancestors.
- **Feeding ecology:** Requires a high-protein diet to fuel its active lifestyle and muscular frame.

## 6. Behaviour / Ecology

- **Social structure:** Exceptionally friendly and outgoing; bonds deeply with human families and often thrives in households with other pets.
- **Activity rhythm:** Diurnal (day-active) to flexible, matching its activity to its owners' schedules.
- **Territory:** Highly curious and investigative within its home territory, enjoying high perches.
- **Ecological role:** Historically protected food supplies from grain-stealing rodents; today, it serves primarily as a companion animal.

## 7. Build & Scale

- **Body size:** Medium to large with a stocky, barrel-shaped body and heavy bone structure.
- **Weight range:** **6.8–9 kg** (15–20 lb) for males; **4.5–6.8 kg** (10–15 lb) for females.
- **Key proportions:** Strong chest and neck; large rounded paws with heavy tufts of fur between the toes to insulate against ice; large, expressive oval eyes.
- **Movement style:** Surprisingly agile jumper, using powerful hind limbs to leap up to heights that would challenge other domestic breeds.

## 8. Signature Traits

1. **Triple-layered coat:** Features dense underfur, insulation awn hairs, and stiff, water-repellent guard hairs.
2. **Tufted paws:** Large, round paws featuring dense tufts of hair protruding between the toes, acting as snowshoes.
3. **Hypoallergenic qualities:** Often produces lower concentrations of Fel d 1, the primary allergen protein present in feline saliva and dander.
4. **Slow development:** A slow-growing breed that can take up to five years to reach its full physical size and coat density.

## 9. Conservation

- **IUCN status:** Not applicable – domestic species.
- **Population trend:** Rapidly increasing in global popularity.
- **Main threats:** None in the wild; pedigree cats face health risks if bred without screening for genetic conditions like hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (a hereditary heart condition).
- **Conservation notes:** Breed registries maintain strict standards and monitor genetic health to prevent inbreeding, ensuring the rugged health of the original landrace is preserved.
