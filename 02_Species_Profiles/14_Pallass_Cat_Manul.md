# Pallas’s Cat / Manul

> **Flat‑faced cold‑steppe specialist**

## 1. Core Identity

- **Common name:** Pallas’s cat, Manul
- **Alternative names:** Steppe cat
- **Scientific name:** *Otocolobus manul*
- **Taxonomic group:** Subfamily *Felinae*; genus *Otocolobus*
- **Lineage:** Pallas’s cat lineage (distinct within Felinae)
- **Exhibit role:** Steppe/mountain small cat exhibit
- **Main biome:** Central Asian steppes, shrublands and rocky deserts
- **Main hunting style:** Ambush and stalk of small mammals

## 2. Museum Summary

The **Pallas’s cat**, or **manul**, is a small wildcat of the Central Asian steppes.  Adults measure **20–24 in** (51–61 cm) in body length with a **tail 8–10 in** (20–25 cm) and weigh **4–11 pounds** (1.8–5 kg)【247072692998982†L160-L173】.  Its **long, dense fur**, **flattened face**, **round pupils** and **low‑set ears** give it a distinctive appearance【247072692998982†L160-L173】.  The coat varies from tan to grey or orange‑red with a frosted look due to white tips on the hairs【247072692998982†L160-L173】.  Pallas’s cats inhabit shrublands, grasslands, semi‑deserts and rocky areas from Iran through Mongolia to the Tibetan Plateau【247072692998982†L176-L178】.  They are solitary and crepuscular, sheltering in rock crevices during the day and hunting pikas, voles and other small rodents at dawn and dusk【247072692998982†L200-L205】.  The species is listed as **Near Threatened**, with populations declining due to rodent poisoning and habitat loss【247072692998982†L222-L228】.

## 3. Range

- **Continents:** Asia.
- **Countries/regions:** Central Asian countries including Mongolia, China (Tibet and Xinjiang), Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Tajikistan, Uzbekistan, Turkmenistan and Iran【247072692998982†L176-L178】.
- **Range type:** Broad but patchy; populations isolated by mountain ranges and deserts.
- **Range notes:** Found between elevations of 1,000–5,000 m; avoids deep snow and dense forest.

## 4. Habitat

- **Primary habitat:** Steppe grasslands, semi‑deserts and shrublands【247072692998982†L176-L178】.
- **Secondary habitat:** Rocky outcrops, alpine meadows and dry valleys; uses burrows dug by marmots and foxes.
- **Climate adaptation:** Dense fur and stocky build conserve heat; camouflage blends with rocky terrain.
- **Terrain preference:** Prefers areas with rocky shelter and sparse vegetation; avoids human settlements.

## 5. Diet

- **Main prey:** Small rodents such as pikas, voles, gerbils, hamsters and marmots【247072692998982†L189-L196】.
- **Secondary prey:** Small birds, lizards and insects【247072692998982†L189-L196】.
- **Hunting method:** Stalks and ambushes prey; pounces from short distances; may wait near burrow entrances【247072692998982†L200-L205】.
- **Feeding ecology:** Specialised predator of pikas and voles; population cycles of prey strongly influence cat numbers.

## 6. Behaviour / Ecology

- **Social structure:** Solitary except during the brief breeding season【247072692998982†L200-L205】.
- **Activity rhythm:** Crepuscular; most active at dawn and dusk; rests in caves or burrows during the day【247072692998982†L200-L205】.
- **Territory:** Individuals occupy home ranges of 7–12 km²; males overlap with several females.
- **Ecological role:** Mesopredator of steppe ecosystems, controlling rodent populations.

## 7. Build & Scale

- **Body size:** **20–24 in** (51–61 cm) with a **tail 8–10 in** (20–25 cm)【247072692998982†L160-L173】.
- **Weight range:** **4–11 lb** (1.8–5 kg)【247072692998982†L160-L173】.
- **Key proportions:** Stocky body, short legs, broad head; flattened face and low ears; dense fur up to 5 cm long【247072692998982†L160-L173】.
- **Movement style:** Moves slowly and deliberately; capable of sudden bursts when attacking prey; poor climber.

## 8. Signature Traits

1. **Flat face and round pupils:** Unique among small cats; gives a stern expression【247072692998982†L160-L173】.
2. **Dense, frosted fur:** Long guard hairs with white tips create a frosted appearance【247072692998982†L160-L173】.
3. **Low‑set ears:** Positioned on the sides of the head to remain hidden while stalking【247072692998982†L160-L173】.
4. **High altitude specialist:** Lives in cold, dry environments with thin air and extreme temperatures【247072692998982†L176-L178】.

## 9. Conservation

- **IUCN status:** Near Threatened.
- **Population trend:** Decreasing; estimated **58,000–100,000** individuals remain【247072692998982†L222-L228】.
- **Main threats:** Secondary poisoning from rodent control programmes (cats eat poisoned rodents), habitat degradation from overgrazing, illegal hunting for fur, and climate change【247072692998982†L222-L228】.
- **Conservation notes:** Included in CITES Appendix II; conservation actions include regulating rodenticide use, protecting habitat and engaging local communities.
