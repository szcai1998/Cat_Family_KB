# Geoffroy's Cat

> The adaptable survivor of the southern steppes, equally at home in the marshes or the mountains.

## 1. Core Identity

- **Common name:** Geoffroy's Cat
- **Alternative names:** Geoffroy's ocelot
- **Scientific name:** _Leopardus geoffroyi_
- **Taxonomic group:** Felinae, Leopardus
- **Lineage:** Ocelot lineage
- **Exhibit role:** The Adaptable Survivor
- **Main biome:** Habitat generalist (pampas, dry forests)
- **Main hunting style:** Ground pursuit and ambush

## 2. Museum Summary

Geoffroy’s cat is South America’s answer to the ultimate survivor. While other spotted cats are tied to the fading rainforests, Geoffroy’s cat has conquered the southern half of the continent. From the swampy wetlands of the Pantanal to the freezing, windswept steppes of Patagonia, this small feline thrives where others perish. It is a rugged generalist, known to hunt on the ground, climb trees to sleep, and confidently wade into water to catch fish. Its striking coat, peppered with numerous small black spots, changes colour—from brilliant ochre in the north to silvery grey in the south—perfectly matching its local environment.

## 3. Range

- **Continents:** South America
- **Countries/regions:** Argentina, Chile, Bolivia, Brazil, Paraguay, and Uruguay.
- **Range type:** Continuous and widespread across the southern cone.
- **Range notes:** The most abundant and widespread wild cat in southern South America.

## 4. Habitat

- **Primary habitat:** Pampas (grasslands), dry scrub, open woodlands, and savannas.
- **Secondary habitat:** Marshlands and semi-arid deserts.
- **Climate adaptation:** Tolerant of extreme temperature variations, from subtropical heat to sub-zero Patagonian winters.
- **Terrain preference:** Requires some form of dense ground cover (tall grass or bushes) for hiding.

## 5. Diet

- **Main prey:** Small rodents (mice, guinea pigs) and hares.
- **Secondary prey:** Fish, frogs, birds, and insects.
- **Hunting method:** Terrestrial stalk-and-pounce; opportunistic wading for aquatic prey.
- **Feeding ecology:** Generalist predator; diet shifts dramatically depending on the season and location.

## 6. Behaviour / Ecology

- **Social structure:** Solitary.
- **Activity rhythm:** Primarily nocturnal (active at night).
- **Territory:** Males defend large territories that overlap with several females.
- **Ecological role:** Essential apex predator of small rodents in grassland ecosystems.

## 7. Build & Scale

- **Body size:** 42–66 cm (17–26 in).
- **Weight range:** 2–6 kg (4.4–13.2 lbs).
- **Key proportions:** Stocky, muscular build with a relatively large head and short legs.
- **Movement style:** Purposeful, ground-dwelling stride; capable swimmer.

## 8. Signature Traits

- **Latitudinal Coat Variation:** The base colour of their fur is brownish-yellow in the northern part of their range, but transitions to a silvery-grey in the colder, southern ranges.
- **Semi-Aquatic Tendencies:** They are excellent swimmers and will actively hunt for fish and frogs in shallow marshes.
- **Stand-Up Posture:** Known to stand on their hind legs (like a meerkat) using their tail for balance, to scan tall pampas grass for prey or predators.

## 9. Conservation

- **IUCN status:** Least Concern (LC)
- **Population trend:** Stable.
- **Main threats:** Habitat fragmentation and retaliatory killings by poultry farmers.
- **Conservation notes:** Previously heavily hunted for the fur trade (hundreds of thousands of pelts exported in the 1980s), but populations have successfully stabilized after international bans.
