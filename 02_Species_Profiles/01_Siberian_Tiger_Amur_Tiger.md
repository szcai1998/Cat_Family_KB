# Siberian Tiger / Amur Tiger

> **Ghost of the frozen north, ruling the snow-draped forests of the Far East**

## 1. Core Identity

- **Common name:** Siberian tiger / Amur tiger
- **Alternative names:** Amur tiger, Ussuri tiger
- **Scientific name:** _Panthera tigris altaica_ (traditional); modern classification: _P. tigris tigris_
- **Taxonomic group:** Subfamily _Pantherinae_; genus _Panthera_
- **Lineage:** Tiger lineage
- **Exhibit role:** Big cat exhibit
- **Main biome:** Boreal and temperate forests (taiga)
- **Main hunting style:** Solitary ambush predator

## 2. Museum Summary

Silently drifting through the dense, snow-choked forests of the Russian Far East, a massive phantom of orange and white stalks its prey. This is the **Siberian tiger**, also known as the **Amur tiger**, a legendary titan engineered by evolution to reign over some of the coldest, harshest landscapes on Earth. With paws that act like natural snowshoes and a coat that thickens into a dense thermal shield, this magnificent predator patrols vast territories where temperatures regularly plummet far below freezing.

Rather than relying on short bursts of speed, the Siberian tiger uses the thick cover of birch and pine forests to creep within striking distance of its quarry. It executes a sudden, explosive pounce to overpower formidable prey like wild boars and deer. Despite its size and strength, the species faced near-extinction in the 20th century due to relentless hunting and habitat fragmentation. Today, thanks to heroic conservation efforts, a few hundred of these sovereign cats still rule the northern forests, serving as a powerful symbol of wilderness resilience.

## 3. Range

- **Continents:** Asia
- **Countries/regions:** Primarily eastern Russia (Amur–Ussuri region); small numbers in northeastern China and possibly North Korea.
- **Range type:** Highly fragmented and restricted to remnant forest patches.
- **Range notes:** Historically ranged across northern China, Korea and parts of Mongolia; now confined to the Russian Far East due to habitat loss and persecution.

## 4. Habitat

- **Primary habitat:** Boreal (taiga) and temperate mixed forests with dense understory and abundant ungulate (hoofed mammal) prey.
- **Secondary habitat:** Riverine valleys and mountainous forests.
- **Climate adaptation:** Thick winter fur, large body size and subcutaneous (under-the-skin) fat reserves help conserve heat.
- **Terrain preference:** Prefers large, contiguous forests with cover for stalking prey; avoids open areas.

## 5. Diet

- **Main prey:** Wild boar, sika deer and red deer.
- **Secondary prey:** Elk, moose, roe deer, bear cubs, hares and smaller mammals; occasionally fish or birds.
- **Hunting method:** Solitary ambush hunter. Stalks silently through the forest, using snowy terrain and trees as cover, then launches a powerful charge to knock its prey off balance before securing a fatal neck bite.
- **Feeding ecology:** Requires vast territories to ensure sufficient prey; a single large kill can sustain a tiger for several days, during which it will drag the carcass into thick cover and guard it.

## 6. Behaviour / Ecology

- **Social structure:** Solitary. Males and females maintain overlapping territories, communicating via scent marks and deep vocalizations; juveniles disperse when mature.
- **Activity rhythm:** Crepuscular (active at dawn and dusk) to nocturnal (night-active); may hunt during the day in areas with minimal human disturbance.
- **Territory:** Males control territories spanning hundreds of square kilometres; females occupy smaller ranges nested within a male's territory.
- **Ecological role:** Apex predator (the top hunter with no natural enemies) regulating populations of herbivores and smaller carnivores to maintain forest health.

## 7. Build & Scale

- **Body size:** Males up to **3.3 m** (10.75 ft) including tail; females slightly shorter.
- **Weight range:** Males **160–190 kg**; females **110–130 kg**.
- **Key proportions:** Broad head with a thick facial ruff; massive shoulders and heavily muscled forelimbs; relatively short tail compared with body length.
- **Movement style:** Power-driven sprinter built for short, explosive charges rather than sustained chases; an exceptionally strong swimmer.

## 8. Signature Traits

1. **Thick winter coat:** Lighter than other tiger populations, with narrow black stripes and dense underfur that acts as a thermal blanket.
2. **Massive build:** The largest and heaviest of all wild cat species, possessing immense skeletal and muscular strength.
3. **Snowshoe paws:** Exceptionally wide paws with thick fur pads that distribute weight evenly, allowing the tiger to walk on deep snow without sinking.
4. **Vast territory patrols:** Navigates search areas larger than almost any other land predator to find food in prey-scarce regions.

## 9. Conservation

- **IUCN status:** Endangered (as part of the mainland tiger subspecies).
- **Population trend:** Slowly increasing; estimated **265–486** individuals remain in the Russian Far East.
- **Main threats:** Poaching for body parts used in traditional medicine, habitat loss from logging and agriculture, depletion of prey populations, and human–tiger conflict.
- **Conservation notes:** Russia’s anti-poaching patrols, strict legal protections, and cross-border cooperation with China have stabilized the population. Developing ecological corridors (protected travel pathways) is key to preventing inbreeding.
