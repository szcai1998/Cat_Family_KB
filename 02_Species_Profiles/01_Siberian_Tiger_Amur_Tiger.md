# Siberian Tiger / Amur Tiger

> **Cold‑climate taiga apex predator**

## 1. Core Identity

- **Common name:** Siberian tiger / Amur tiger
- **Alternative names:** Amur tiger, Ussuri tiger
- **Scientific name:** *Panthera tigris altaica* (traditional); modern classification: *P. t. tigris*
- **Taxonomic group:** Subfamily *Pantherinae*; genus *Panthera*
- **Lineage:** Tiger lineage
- **Exhibit role:** Big cat exhibit
- **Main biome:** Boreal and temperate forests (taiga)
- **Main hunting style:** Solitary ambush predator

## 2. Museum Summary

The **Siberian tiger**, also called the **Amur tiger**, is the largest living cat.  Males can reach around **10.75 feet** (3.3 m) from nose to tail and weigh up to **660 pounds** (300 kg)【960819529586131†L75-L104】.  Their bodies are powerfully built and covered with a **pale orange coat** with **narrow black stripes**.  A **thick fur and dense undercoat** insulate them against the harsh winters of the Russian Far East【362655593780840†L60-L114】.  These apex predators roam vast territories, stalking deer and wild boar through birch and mixed forests.  Hunting and habitat loss nearly wiped them out in the 20th century, but intensive conservation has stabilised the population at a few hundred individuals【362655593780840†L88-L123】.

## 3. Range

- **Continents:** Asia
- **Countries/regions:** Primarily eastern Russia (Amur–Ussuri region); small numbers in northeastern China and possibly North Korea【960819529586131†L75-L104】.
- **Range type:** Highly fragmented and restricted to remnant forest patches.
- **Range notes:** Historically ranged across northern China, Korea and parts of Mongolia; now confined to the Russian Far East due to habitat loss and persecution.

## 4. Habitat

- **Primary habitat:** Boreal (taiga) and temperate mixed forests with dense understory and abundant ungulate prey【960819529586131†L75-L104】.
- **Secondary habitat:** Riverine valleys and mountainous forests.
- **Climate adaptation:** Thick winter fur, large body size and fat reserves help conserve heat【362655593780840†L60-L114】.
- **Terrain preference:** Prefers large, contiguous forests with cover for stalking prey; avoids open areas.

## 5. Diet

- **Main prey:** Wild boar, sika deer and red deer【362655593780840†L88-L123】.
- **Secondary prey:** Elk, moose, roe deer, bear cubs, hares and smaller mammals; occasionally fish or birds.
- **Hunting method:** Solitary ambush hunter.  Tigers silently stalk prey and launch a powerful charge to knock animals off balance.  They may drag carcasses to cover and feed over several days.
- **Feeding ecology:** Requires large territories to ensure sufficient prey; a single kill can sustain a tiger for several days.

## 6. Behaviour / Ecology

- **Social structure:** Solitary.  Males and females maintain overlapping territories; juveniles disperse when mature.
- **Activity rhythm:** Crepuscular to nocturnal; may be more diurnal in undisturbed areas.
- **Territory:** Males control territories of hundreds of square kilometres; females occupy smaller ranges within male territories.
- **Ecological role:** Apex predator regulating populations of ungulates and smaller carnivores.

## 7. Build & Scale

- **Body size:** Males up to **10.75 ft** (3.3 m) including tail【960819529586131†L75-L104】; females slightly shorter.
- **Weight range:** Males **160–190 kg**; females **110–130 kg**【362655593780840†L60-L114】.
- **Key proportions:** Broad head with long whiskers; massive forelimbs and shoulders; relatively short tail compared with body length.
- **Movement style:** Powerful but not built for sustained speed.  Can sprint short distances and swim well.

## 8. Signature Traits

1. **Thick, pale coat:** Lighter than other tiger subspecies, with narrow stripes and dense underfur for cold climates【362655593780840†L60-L114】.
2. **Large size:** Heaviest and longest of all cat species, giving it great strength【960819529586131†L75-L104】.
3. **Long whiskers and facial ruff:** Help detect obstacles and regulate body temperature in snow.
4. **Large paws:** Spread weight on snow like snowshoes.

## 9. Conservation

- **IUCN status:** Endangered (as part of the mainland tiger subspecies).  Regional Amur tiger population is managed separately.
- **Population trend:** Slowly increasing; estimated **265–486** individuals remain in the Russian Far East【362655593780840†L88-L123】.
- **Main threats:** Poaching for body parts, habitat loss from logging and agriculture, depletion of prey and human–tiger conflict【960819529586131†L75-L104】.
- **Conservation notes:** Russia’s anti‑poaching patrols, legal protections and cross‑border cooperation with China have improved prospects.  Habitat corridors are being developed to connect isolated populations.
