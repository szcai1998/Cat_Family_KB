# Pampas Cat

> The fierce, coarse-furred wanderer of the windswept Patagonian grasslands.

## 1. Core Identity

- **Common name:** Pampas Cat
- **Alternative names:** Pantanal Cat, Colocolo
- **Scientific name:** _Leopardus colocola_
- **Taxonomic group:** Felinae, Leopardus
- **Lineage:** Ocelot lineage
- **Exhibit role:** The Grassland Wanderer
- **Main biome:** Grasslands (pampas), shrublands, and high Andes
- **Main hunting style:** Ground pursuit and pounce

## 2. Museum Summary

The Pampas cat is a rugged, heavy-set feline built for the harsh, open environments of South America. Although its name implies it is restricted to the grassy plains (pampas) of Argentina, it is actually a remarkable generalist, roaming from the swampy Pantanal to the high altitudes of the Andes. It has a distinctive, coarse coat that it can bristle to make itself look incredibly massive and intimidating when threatened. Depending on where it lives, its fur can range from heavily spotted and striped to almost completely uniform in colour, adapting to blend perfectly with the local scrub or golden grass.

## 3. Range

- **Continents:** South America
- **Countries/regions:** Argentina, Chile, Bolivia, Peru, Ecuador, and southwestern Brazil.
- **Range type:** Widespread but patchy.
- **Range notes:** The extreme variation in its appearance across its vast range has led some scientists to suggest it might actually be three or more distinct species.

## 4. Habitat

- **Primary habitat:** Grasslands (pampas), semi-arid scrublands, and open woodlands.
- **Secondary habitat:** High-altitude rocky deserts and marshlands (Pantanal).
- **Climate adaptation:** Extremely cold-hardy with thick, insulating fur.
- **Terrain preference:** Prefers open areas with scattered cover rather than dense forests.

## 5. Diet

- **Main prey:** Small rodents (guinea pigs, vizcachas) and ground-dwelling birds (tinamous).
- **Secondary prey:** Lizards and insects.
- **Hunting method:** Terrestrial stalk-and-pounce.
- **Feeding ecology:** Highly dependent on local rodent populations; often hunts near the burrows of its prey.

## 6. Behaviour / Ecology

- **Social structure:** Solitary.
- **Activity rhythm:** Primarily nocturnal (night-active), but frequently seen hunting during the day in high-altitude regions.
- **Territory:** Large home ranges necessary due to the sparse prey density in arid grasslands.
- **Ecological role:** Crucial mesopredator controlling rodent populations in open ecosystems.

## 7. Build & Scale

- **Body size:** 46–75 cm (18–30 in) body length.
- **Weight range:** 3–7 kg (6.6–15.4 lbs).
- **Key proportions:** Stocky build with a relatively broad face and short, thick legs.
- **Movement style:** Deliberate, low-slung walk through tall grass.

## 8. Signature Traits

- **Piloerection Defense:** When threatened, a Pampas cat can stand its exceptionally long, coarse fur on end (piloerection), instantly doubling its apparent size to intimidate predators like foxes or dogs.
- **Extreme Morphological Variation:** Its coat pattern is one of the most variable of any wild cat. Some are heavily marked with dark rosettes, while others are entirely rusty-red or silvery-grey with almost no visible spots.
- **High-Altitude Tolerance:** They have been recorded living at staggering elevations up to 5,000 meters in the Andes.

## 9. Conservation

- **IUCN status:** Near Threatened (NT)
- **Population trend:** Decreasing.
- **Main threats:** Habitat destruction from agricultural expansion (soy and cattle) and retaliatory killing for preying on poultry.
- **Conservation notes:** Protecting the remaining native grasslands is vital, as the pampas ecosystem is one of the most heavily modified habitats in South America.
