# Eurasian Lynx

> **Forest/mountain ambush hunter**

## 1. Core Identity

- **Common name:** Eurasian lynx
- **Alternative names:** European lynx
- **Scientific name:** *Lynx lynx*
- **Taxonomic group:** Subfamily *Felinae*; genus *Lynx*
- **Lineage:** Lynx lineage
- **Exhibit role:** Lynx exhibit
- **Main biome:** Boreal forests, mixed forests and mountains of Europe and Asia
- **Main hunting style:** Ambush predator

## 2. Museum Summary

The **Eurasian lynx** is the largest of the lynx species and one of the widest‑ranging wild cats.  Adults weigh **18–36 kg (39.7–79.3 lb)** and measure **70–130 cm** in length【843266011844487†L100-L114】【843266011844487†L131-L138】.  They are characterised by **large, tufted ears**, a **flared facial ruff** and **large paws** that act as snowshoes【843266011844487†L100-L114】.  Coat colour varies from pale grey to rich reddish‑brown with dark spots.  Eurasian lynxes inhabit forests and mountains from western Europe across Russia to Central Asia and the Tibetan Plateau【843266011844487†L60-L70】.  They are solitary ambush hunters that prey on deer and smaller mammals.  Unlike smaller lynx species, the Eurasian lynx can take sizeable ungulates such as roe deer.

## 3. Range

- **Continents:** Europe and Asia.
- **Countries/regions:** Western and Northern Europe, Russia, the Caucasus, Siberia, Mongolia and the Tibetan Plateau【843266011844487†L60-L70】.
- **Range type:** Broad but fragmented by human settlement and habitat loss.
- **Range notes:** Reintroduction programmes have restored lynx to parts of Central Europe.  Populations in western Europe remain small and isolated.

## 4. Habitat

- **Primary habitat:** Forests—both boreal coniferous and temperate deciduous【843266011844487†L78-L93】.
- **Secondary habitat:** Scrublands, rocky hills, open woodland and mountainous terrain【843266011844487†L78-L93】.
- **Climate adaptation:** Dense fur and large paws enable travel on snow; seasonal coat changes from thick winter fur to shorter summer fur.
- **Terrain preference:** Requires cover and rugged terrain for stalking; avoids open plains.

## 5. Diet

- **Main prey:** Small and medium ungulates such as roe deer【843266011844487†L78-L93】.
- **Secondary prey:** Hares, rabbits, rodents and birds.
- **Hunting method:** Ambush predator; uses stealth and pounces on prey from close range.
- **Feeding ecology:** Solitary feeder; may cache unused portions and return later.

## 6. Behaviour / Ecology

- **Social structure:** Solitary; adults come together only for mating.  Females raise kittens alone.
- **Activity rhythm:** Primarily crepuscular and nocturnal; activity peaks around dawn and dusk.
- **Territory:** Home ranges vary from 20 km² to over 300 km²; males range wider than females and overlap with multiple females.
- **Ecological role:** Controls populations of herbivores and small mammals; interactions with prey influence forest regeneration.

## 7. Build & Scale

- **Body size:** **70–130 cm** body length; shoulder height **60–65 cm**【843266011844487†L100-L114】【843266011844487†L131-L138】.
- **Weight range:** **18–36 kg (39.7–79.3 lb)**【843266011844487†L131-L138】.
- **Key proportions:** Long legs, short body, stubby tail (< 20 cm); long ear tufts and facial ruff【843266011844487†L100-L114】.
- **Movement style:** Stealthy and agile; capable of leaping long distances and moving silently through snow.

## 8. Signature Traits

1. **Ear tufts:** Black tufts up to 4 cm long enhance hearing and aid camouflage【843266011844487†L100-L114】.
2. **Facial ruff:** Flared whisker pads create a characteristic broad face and may help funnel sound【843266011844487†L100-L114】.
3. **Large paws:** Function like snowshoes, supporting weight on snow【843266011844487†L100-L114】.
4. **Variable coat:** Colour and spotting vary by region and season.

## 9. Conservation

- **IUCN status:** Least Concern globally, though some regional populations are Near Threatened.
- **Population trend:** Stable to increasing in parts of northern Europe; declining in areas with habitat loss and hunting【843266011844487†L60-L70】.
- **Main threats:** Habitat fragmentation, poaching for fur and trophy, conflict with livestock farmers, and road mortality.
- **Conservation notes:** Protected under CITES; reintroduction and translocation programmes have restored populations in Switzerland, Austria and Slovenia.  Connectivity and prey availability are crucial for long‑term viability.
