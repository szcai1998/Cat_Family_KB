# Snow Leopard

> **High‑altitude cold‑adapted predator**

## 1. Core Identity

- **Common name:** Snow leopard
- **Alternative names:** Ounce
- **Scientific name:** *Panthera uncia*
- **Taxonomic group:** Subfamily *Pantherinae*; genus *Panthera*
- **Lineage:** Snow leopard lineage within Panthera
- **Exhibit role:** Mountain cat exhibit
- **Main biome:** Alpine and subalpine mountains of Central Asia
- **Main hunting style:** Ambush predator on steep terrain

## 2. Museum Summary

The **snow leopard** inhabits some of the highest and coldest mountains on Earth.  Adults measure about **2.1 m (7 ft)** in total length, including a **0.9 m (3 ft) tail**, and weigh **23–41 kg (50–90 lb)**【596547831032212†L233-L265】.  Their thick, greyish fur is patterned with dark rosettes and spots, and a dense woolly undercoat provides insulation【596547831032212†L233-L265】.  Snow leopards are elusive ambush hunters, preying on wild sheep, ibex and marmots across vast territories in the Himalayas and Central Asian ranges.  Their long tail aids balance on rocky slopes and serves as a wrap against the cold.  Formerly classified in their own genus (*Uncia*), genetic studies now place them within *Panthera*.  The species is listed as **Vulnerable**, with an estimated 2,500–10,000 mature individuals and a decreasing trend【596547831032212†L248-L286】.

## 3. Range

- **Continents:** Asia
- **Countries/regions:** Mountain ranges of Central and South Asia including the Himalayas (India, Nepal, Bhutan), the Karakoram and Pamir ranges, the Tibetan Plateau, Mongolia and southern Siberia【596547831032212†L233-L265】.
- **Range type:** Discontinuous; populations are scattered across isolated mountain massifs.
- **Range notes:** Occurs at elevations between 1,500 m and 5,500 m; snowline shifts and habitat fragmentation affect connectivity.

## 4. Habitat

- **Primary habitat:** Rocky alpine and subalpine zones with cliffs, ridges and scree【596547831032212†L233-L265】.
- **Secondary habitat:** Shrublands and montane forests at lower elevations where prey descends seasonally.
- **Climate adaptation:** Dense fur and thick tail provide insulation; nasal cavities large for warming cold air.
- **Terrain preference:** Steep, rugged terrain that offers cover and high observation points; avoids human settlements.

## 5. Diet

- **Main prey:** Wild sheep and goats such as bharal (blue sheep), ibex and argali【596547831032212†L248-L286】.
- **Secondary prey:** Marmots, pikas, hares and domestic livestock (sheep, goats, yaks)【596547831032212†L248-L286】.
- **Hunting method:** Ambush predator; stalks from above and leaps to seize prey, often chasing animals down steep slopes.
- **Feeding ecology:** Requires large territories with adequate prey; will cache remains and return later.

## 6. Behaviour / Ecology

- **Social structure:** Solitary except during mating season and when females raise cubs.
- **Activity rhythm:** Crepuscular, with peaks at dawn and dusk; may be active during the day in undisturbed areas.
- **Territory:** Adults occupy ranges of 30–1,000 km² depending on prey density; males overlap with multiple females.
- **Ecological role:** Apex predator in mountain ecosystems; controls populations of wild sheep and goats.

## 7. Build & Scale

- **Body size:** **2.1 m (7 ft)** including a **0.9 m (3 ft) tail**【596547831032212†L233-L265】.
- **Weight range:** **23–41 kg (50–90 lb)**【596547831032212†L233-L265】.
- **Key proportions:** Long tail (almost half body length) for balance and warmth; large paws covered in fur act like snowshoes.
- **Movement style:** Agile climber and jumper; can leap up to 15 m across ravines.

## 8. Signature Traits

1. **Thick fur and woolly undercoat:** Insulates against cold; fur colour camouflages with rocky terrain【596547831032212†L233-L265】.
2. **Long, bushy tail:** Aids balance on cliffs and wraps around the body when resting【596547831032212†L233-L265】.
3. **Large nasal cavity:** Warms cold, thin air at high altitudes.
4. **Powerful hind limbs:** Enable long leaps across chasms.

## 9. Conservation

- **IUCN status:** Vulnerable (downlisted from Endangered in 2017).
- **Population trend:** Decreasing; estimated **2,500–10,000** mature individuals remain【596547831032212†L248-L286】.
- **Main threats:** Decline of wild prey, retaliatory killings by herders when snow leopards prey on livestock, poaching for bones and skins【596547831032212†L248-L286】.
- **Conservation notes:** Initiatives include community‑based conservation programmes, compensation schemes for livestock loss, anti‑poaching enforcement and research to map populations.
