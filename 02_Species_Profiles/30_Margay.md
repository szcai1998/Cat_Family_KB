# Margay

> The acrobatic master of the rainforest canopy, performing high-wire hunts in the dark.

## 1. Core Identity

- **Common name:** Margay
- **Alternative names:** Tree ocelot, long-tailed spotted cat
- **Scientific name:** _Leopardus wiedii_
- **Taxonomic group:** Felinae, Leopardus
- **Lineage:** Ocelot lineage
- **Exhibit role:** The Canopy Acrobat
- **Main biome:** Tropical/subtropical evergreen forests
- **Main hunting style:** Arboreal ambush

## 2. Museum Summary

Known as the "tree ocelot," the margay is an unparalleled aerialist. While most cats retreat to the ground to hunt, the margay practically lives in the canopy. It possesses a remarkable anatomical adaptation: specially modified ankle joints that can rotate 180 degrees. This unique joint allows the margay to scurry headfirst down tree trunks like a squirrel and even hang by a single hind paw while grasping prey with its front claws. Wrapped in a stunning coat of dark rosettes, this small cat embodies the wild verticality of the Neotropical rainforests.

## 3. Range

- **Continents:** South America, North America (Central America)
- **Countries/regions:** From Mexico down through Central America to northern South America, extending into Brazil and Paraguay.
- **Range type:** Patchy but widespread in suitable forest.
- **Range notes:** Its distribution is highly dependent on continuous forest cover; fragmentation severely isolates populations.

## 4. Habitat

- **Primary habitat:** Tropical and subtropical evergreen forests, cloud forests.
- **Secondary habitat:** Montane forests and shaded coffee plantations (if densely vegetated).
- **Climate adaptation:** Highly adapted to humid, multi-layered environments.
- **Terrain preference:** Strictly arboreal (tree-dwelling); rarely seen on the open ground.

## 5. Diet

- **Main prey:** Arboreal mammals (small monkeys, squirrels, tree frogs, sloths).
- **Secondary prey:** Birds, insects, and occasionally fruit.
- **Hunting method:** Arboreal stalk-and-pounce; uses extreme agility to catch fast-moving prey in the canopy.
- **Feeding ecology:** Highly specialized for hunting off the ground, a niche untouched by heavier cats like the ocelot.

## 6. Behaviour / Ecology

- **Social structure:** Solitary.
- **Activity rhythm:** Strictly nocturnal (active at night).
- **Territory:** Ranges overlap heavily, but individuals avoid each other.
- **Ecological role:** Mesopredator regulating arboreal mammal and bird populations.

## 7. Build & Scale

- **Body size:** 48–79 cm (19–31 in) body, with a tail up to 51 cm (20 in).
- **Weight range:** 2.6–4 kg (5.7–8.8 lbs).
- **Key proportions:** Extremely long tail used as a counterweight for balancing on thin branches; large eyes for night vision.
- **Movement style:** Acrobatic climbing, capable of jumping great distances between branches.

## 8. Signature Traits

- **180-Degree Rotating Ankles:** Supination (outward rotation) of the hind feet allows it to climb down trees headfirst.
- **Counterweight Tail:** A tail that can make up to 70% of its body length, acting as a pendulum for balance.
- **Vocal Mimicry:** Documented mimicking the distress call of a baby pied tamarin monkey to lure adult monkeys into an ambush.

## 9. Conservation

- **IUCN status:** Near Threatened (NT)
- **Population trend:** Decreasing.
- **Main threats:** Deforestation (it will not cross cleared land), illegal pet trade, and retaliatory killing.
- **Conservation notes:** Protecting continuous forest corridors is the only way to save the margay, as it relies on interconnected tree canopies.
