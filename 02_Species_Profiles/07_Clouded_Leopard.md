# Clouded Leopard

> **Arboreal forest cat with long canines**

## 1. Core Identity

- **Common name:** Clouded leopard
- **Alternative names:** Mainland clouded leopard
- **Scientific name:** *Neofelis nebulosa*
- **Taxonomic group:** Subfamily *Pantherinae*; genus *Neofelis*
- **Lineage:** Clouded leopard lineage within Pantherinae
- **Exhibit role:** Forest cat exhibit
- **Main biome:** Tropical and subtropical forests of Southeast Asia
- **Main hunting style:** Arboreal ambush and pouncing

## 2. Museum Summary

The **clouded leopard** is a secretive cat of Asian forests, named for its large cloud‑like spots.  Adults measure **2–3 ft** (60–90 cm) in body length with tails up to **3 ft** (90 cm) and can weigh **up to 50 pounds** (23 kg)【438996514644063†L41-L96】.  Their most striking feature is their disproportionately long **upper canine teeth**, among the longest relative to skull size of any cat, reminiscent of sabre‑toothed cats【438996514644063†L41-L96】.  Clouded leopards are superb climbers: short, powerful legs, large paws and a reversible ankle joint allow them to climb down trees headfirst and hang from branches【438996514644063†L41-L96】.  They inhabit forests from Nepal and northeastern India through Southeast Asia to southern China.  The species is listed as **Vulnerable** and threatened by habitat loss and poaching.

## 3. Range

- **Continents:** Asia
- **Countries/regions:** Forests of the eastern Himalayas, northeastern India, Bhutan, Myanmar, Thailand, Laos, Vietnam, Cambodia, Malaysia, Indonesia and southern China【438996514644063†L41-L96】.
- **Range type:** Patchy and shrinking due to deforestation.
- **Range notes:** Two species of clouded leopard are now recognised: the mainland clouded leopard (_N. nebulosa_) and the Sunda clouded leopard (_N. diardi_), the latter confined to Borneo and Sumatra (not covered here).

## 4. Habitat

- **Primary habitat:** Tropical and subtropical evergreen forests including lowland rainforests and montane forests【438996514644063†L41-L96】.
- **Secondary habitat:** Mangroves, grasslands and scrub adjacent to forests.
- **Climate adaptation:** Thick, patterned fur provides camouflage in dappled forest light.
- **Terrain preference:** Prefers areas with large trees for climbing and resting; avoids open landscapes.

## 5. Diet

- **Main prey:** Ungulates such as deer and pigs, and primates (monkeys)【438996514644063†L41-L96】.
- **Secondary prey:** Squirrels, birds and occasionally livestock【438996514644063†L41-L96】.
- **Hunting method:** Primarily ambushes prey from trees or dense undergrowth; can pounce down from branches using its agility.
- **Feeding ecology:** Solitary hunter; caches prey in trees or dense vegetation.

## 6. Behaviour / Ecology

- **Social structure:** Solitary, except during breeding season and mother–young pairs.
- **Activity rhythm:** Mostly nocturnal or crepuscular; seldom observed in the wild.
- **Territory:** Range size unknown but presumed similar to other medium cats; both males and females scent‑mark trees.
- **Ecological role:** Mesopredator within forest ecosystems; controls populations of arboreal and terrestrial prey.

## 7. Build & Scale

- **Body size:** **2–3 ft** (60–90 cm) body with up to **3 ft** (90 cm) tail【438996514644063†L41-L96】.
- **Weight range:** Up to **50 lb** (23 kg)【438996514644063†L41-L96】.
- **Key proportions:** Short limbs and long tail; elongated canines up to 4 cm; flexible ankle joints that rotate to climb headfirst.【438996514644063†L41-L96】.
- **Movement style:** Highly arboreal; climbs and leaps between branches; capable of hanging upside down and descending trunks headfirst.

## 8. Signature Traits

1. **Cloud‑shaped spots:** Large, irregular blotches outlined in black give the species its name.
2. **Elongated canines:** Proportionally longest among extant cats【438996514644063†L41-L96】.
3. **Reversible ankles and flexible joints:** Allow descent headfirst and climbing along branches【438996514644063†L41-L96】.
4. **Arboreal lifestyle:** Spends much time in trees; rarely comes to the ground compared to other big cats.

## 9. Conservation

- **IUCN status:** Vulnerable.
- **Population trend:** Decreasing due to deforestation, habitat fragmentation and poaching for fur and bones【438996514644063†L41-L96】.
- **Main threats:** Loss of forest habitat from logging and agriculture, hunting for pelts, and capture for the pet trade.【438996514644063†L41-L96】.
- **Conservation notes:** Protective measures include establishing and connecting forest reserves, enforcing anti‑poaching laws and community education.
