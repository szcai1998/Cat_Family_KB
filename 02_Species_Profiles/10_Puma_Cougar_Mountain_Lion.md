# Puma / Cougar / Mountain Lion

> **Silent athlete of the Americas**

## 1. Core Identity

- **Common name:** Puma, cougar, mountain lion
- **Alternative names:** Panther, catamount, painter; Guinness World Record holder for most names【380882063012250†L104-L165】
- **Scientific name:** *Puma concolor*
- **Taxonomic group:** Subfamily *Felinae*; genus *Puma*
- **Lineage:** Puma lineage (shared with the cheetah)
- **Exhibit role:** American cat exhibit
- **Main biome:** Wide range including forests, mountains, deserts and grasslands
- **Main hunting style:** Ambush predator with powerful leaps

## 2. Museum Summary

The **puma** is one of the most adaptable large cats, ranging from the Canadian Yukon to the Andes of southern Chile【671062261146697†L245-L265】.  Adult males measure **6–8 ft** (1.8–2.4 m) in length (including tail) and weigh **50–80 kg**, while females are **5–7 ft** (1.5–2.1 m) long and weigh **35–60 kg**【380882063012250†L104-L165】.  Pumas have a **uniform tawny coat**, a small head, long neck and a long tail.  Their powerful hind legs enable them to leap more than **6 m** vertically and cover **12 m** horizontally【380882063012250†L159-L182】.  They are solitary ambush predators that feed mainly on deer but will take prey ranging from rabbits to elk.  Pumas are classified as **Near Threatened**, with fewer than 50,000 mature individuals and a declining population【380882063012250†L104-L165】.

## 3. Range

- **Continents:** North America and South America【671062261146697†L245-L265】.
- **Countries/regions:** From Canada’s Yukon through the western United States, Mexico, Central America and across South America to Patagonia【671062261146697†L245-L265】.
- **Range type:** Continuous across remote mountainous and forested areas but fragmented in populated regions; extirpated from most of eastern North America.
- **Range notes:** Occupies the widest latitudinal range of any New World mammal.

## 4. Habitat

- **Primary habitat:** Forests (coniferous, deciduous, tropical) and mountainous regions【671062261146697†L245-L265】.
- **Secondary habitat:** Deserts, chaparral, swamps and open grasslands【671062261146697†L245-L265】.
- **Climate adaptation:** Thick winter coat in northern ranges; in the tropics, shorter fur.  Adaptable to elevations from sea level to alpine zones.
- **Terrain preference:** Prefers areas with cover for stalking; avoids wide open plains unless adjacent to cover.

## 5. Diet

- **Main prey:** Ungulates such as deer, elk, mule deer, white‑tailed deer and guanacos【671062261146697†L245-L265】.
- **Secondary prey:** Smaller mammals (rabbits, hares, rodents), birds, domestic livestock and occasionally coyotes【671062261146697†L269-L279】.
- **Hunting method:** Ambushes from close range, using stealth and powerful leaps; kills by biting the neck.  Travels **10 km or more per night** in search of prey【671062261146697†L312-L318】.
- **Feeding ecology:** Highly opportunistic; caches large kills and covers them with leaves or snow; returns to feed over several days.

## 6. Behaviour / Ecology

- **Social structure:** Solitary except during mating.  Mothers raise cubs for up to two years.
- **Activity rhythm:** Mostly crepuscular or nocturnal; may hunt during the day in remote areas or when prey are active.
- **Territory:** Females’ home ranges average **~140 km²**, while males’ territories can be double this size【671062261146697†L312-L318】.
- **Ecological role:** Apex or mesopredator depending on region; influences prey populations and competes with wolves and bears where ranges overlap.

## 7. Build & Scale

- **Body size:** Males **6–8 ft** (1.8–2.4 m) including tail; females **5–7 ft** (1.5–2.1 m)【380882063012250†L104-L165】.
- **Weight range:** Males **50–80 kg**; females **35–60 kg**【380882063012250†L104-L165】.
- **Key proportions:** Uniformly coloured coat, small head, long body and tail; hind legs longer than front legs enabling extraordinary leaps【380882063012250†L159-L182】.
- **Movement style:** Stealthy stalking and short bursts of speed; excellent climber and jumper; can swim when necessary.

## 8. Signature Traits

1. **Uniform coat:** Lacks spots or stripes; fur colour ranges from silver‑grey in high latitudes to reddish‑brown in tropics.【671062261146697†L255-L263】
2. **Powerful hind legs:** Allow jumps up to 6 m high and 12 m long【380882063012250†L159-L182】.
3. **Most names:** Holds a Guinness World Record for the most names for any animal, reflecting its broad distribution and cultural significance【380882063012250†L104-L165】.
4. **Flexible vocal repertoire:** Can hiss, growl, scream and purr but cannot roar.

## 9. Conservation

- **IUCN status:** Near Threatened.
- **Population trend:** Decreasing; fewer than 50,000 mature individuals remain【380882063012250†L104-L165】.
- **Main threats:** Habitat loss and fragmentation, persecution for livestock predation, hunting for sport and fur, road mortality.
- **Conservation notes:** Protected in many countries; corridors and landscape connectivity vital for dispersal.  In parts of the United States, puma populations are rebounding where protection measures are strong.
