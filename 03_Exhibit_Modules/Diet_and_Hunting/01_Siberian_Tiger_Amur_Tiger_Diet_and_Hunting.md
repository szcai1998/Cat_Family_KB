# Siberian Tiger – Diet and Hunting

## Overview

> A phantom in the snow, tracking the heavy footsteps of wild boar through the freezing, silent taiga.

The dietary ecology of the Siberian Tiger (_Panthera tigris altaica_) is a testament to its status as a hypercarnivore (a diet consisting of over 70% meat). Its hunting strategies are dictated by the deep snow and low prey density of the Russian Far East.

## Key Information

- **Diet Composition:** Strictly carnivorous.
- **Prey Preferences:** Large ungulates (hoofed mammals) make up the vast majority of its diet.
  - Wild boar and red deer (sambar) account for up to 80% of its intake.
  - Secondary prey includes sika deer, roe deer, and occasionally smaller brown bears.
- **Intake Requirements:** Consumes 2–5% of its body weight daily, requiring frequent large kills.
- **Hunting Strategy:** Solitary ambush predator. Relies on dense forest cover to get within 10–25 meters before launching a lethal sprint.

## Interpretation and Comparisons

Unlike the African Lion, which uses cooperative hunting to take down massive prey in open spaces, the Siberian tiger relies entirely on stealth and explosive power. Its massive size is a necessity to physically overpower large ungulates single-handedly in deep snow.
