# Bengal Tiger – Diet and Hunting

## Overview

> An unstoppable force of muscle and teeth, engineered to bring down prey twice its size through sheer blunt trauma.

The dietary ecology of the Bengal Tiger (_Panthera tigris tigris_) relies entirely on its ability to ambush and overpower large, dangerous ungulates single-handedly.

## Key Information

- **Diet Composition:** Strictly carnivorous.
- **Prey Preferences:** Exclusively hunts medium to massive ungulates.
  - Primary targets: Chital (spotted deer), sambar deer, and wild boar.
  - Secondary targets: Massive bovids like the gaur (Indian bison) and nilgai. They will also occasionally hunt water buffalo.
- **Hunting Strategy:** A solitary stalk-and-ambush predator. They use the tall grass to creep within a few meters of their prey before launching a terrifyingly fast sprint.
- **The Killing Bite:** They kill either by snapping the neck (for smaller prey) or delivering a sustained, suffocating bite to the throat (for larger prey) that can last up to 10 minutes.

## Interpretation and Comparisons

While lions can rely on the collective weight of their pride to drag down a massive African buffalo, a Bengal tiger must tackle the equally massive gaur entirely alone. To do this, the tiger relies on the element of surprise and its immense forelimb strength to throw the animal off balance before securing a fatal throat grip.
