# Snow Leopard – Diet and Hunting

## Overview

> A master of gravity, launching itself down vertical cliffs to strike mountain sheep before they can react.

The dietary ecology of the Snow Leopard (_Panthera uncia_) revolves around hunting extremely agile prey on treacherous, nearly vertical terrain.

## Key Information

- **Diet Composition:** Specialized carnivore.
- **Prey Preferences:** Heavily relies on wild mountain ungulates in the 36–76 kg range.
  - Primary targets: Blue sheep (bharal), Siberian ibex, and argali.
  - Secondary targets: Marmots, pikas, hares, and galliform birds (like snowcocks).
- **Livestock Predation:** Depending on the region, domestic livestock (sheep, goats, yaks) can constitute 0–33% of their diet, particularly where wild prey has been overhunted.
- **Hunting Strategy:** A solitary ambush predator. They typically attack from above, using the element of surprise to chase prey down steep slopes in a series of massive, bounding leaps.

## Interpretation and Comparisons

Snow leopards are incredibly athletic hunters. While a cheetah uses flat ground to reach maximum speed, a snow leopard uses gravity and momentum, sometimes tumbling hundreds of feet down a mountainside while locked onto its prey. They are capable of killing prey up to three times their own weight.
