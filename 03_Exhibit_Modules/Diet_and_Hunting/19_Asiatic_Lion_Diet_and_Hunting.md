# Asiatic Lion – Diet and Hunting

## Overview

> Adapting to a crowded forest, the Asiatic lion has learned to hunt what is available, blurring the line between wild predator and domestic nuisance.

The dietary ecology of the Asiatic Lion (_Panthera leo persica_) is a product of its restricted environment. Because the Gir Forest is surrounded by human settlements, their diet heavily reflects human presence.

## Key Information

- **Diet Composition:** Carnivorous, but heavily supplemented by domestic animals.
- **Prey Preferences:**
  - Wild Prey (~64–70% of diet): Chital (spotted deer), sambar deer, and nilgai (large Asian antelope).
  - Domestic Prey (~30–31% of diet): Cattle and buffalo owned by the local Maldhari herders.
- **Hunting Strategy:** Cooperative ambush hunters, though their prides are smaller than African lions, so their hunting parties are generally smaller.

## Interpretation and Comparisons

Unlike the African lion, which rarely eats livestock unless its natural prey is depleted or it is injured, the Asiatic lion routinely preys on domestic cattle. This is primarily because the Maldhari pastoralists live directly within the Gir Forest, bringing the lions' natural prey and domestic livestock into constant, overlapping contact.
