# African Lion – Diet and Hunting

## Overview

> A highly coordinated tactical strike force, capable of toppling a one-ton buffalo through sheer teamwork.

The African Lion (_Panthera leo_) is an obligate carnivore with a hunting strategy unlike any other wild cat. By hunting in cooperative groups, a pride of lions can secure massive amounts of biomass that solitary predators could never dream of tackling.

## Key Information

- **Diet Composition:** Heavily carnivorous (>75% meat). Up to 40% of their intake can actually come from scavenging other predators' kills (like hyenas or cheetahs).
- **Prey Preferences:** Medium-to-large ungulates weighing between 40–250 kg.
  - Primary targets: Wildebeest, zebra, African buffalo, and gemsbok.
  - Secondary targets: Warthogs, impalas, and even young elephants or giraffes in large, desperate prides.
- **Intake Requirements:** A lioness requires about 5 kg of meat per day, while a male requires roughly 7 kg, though they gorge up to 30 kg in a single sitting.
- **Hunting Strategy:** Cooperative ambush. Females perform most of the hunting, flanking herds and driving prey toward hidden, waiting pride members.

## Interpretation and Comparisons

While solitary big cats like the leopard hunt smaller prey to minimize the risk of injury, lions specifically target dangerous megafauna. Their cooperative hunting not only allows them to kill buffalo, but also allows them to fiercely defend their kills against massive clans of spotted hyenas.
