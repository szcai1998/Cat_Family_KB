# Jaguar – Diet and Hunting

## Overview

> The undisputed apex predator of the Neotropics, dealing death with a single, skull-crushing bite.

The dietary ecology of the Jaguar (_Panthera onca_) is characterized by extraordinary opportunism and devastating bite force. It is the only big cat that regularly kills its prey by piercing the skull.

## Key Information

- **Diet Composition:** Strict carnivore. Known to hunt over 85 different species.
- **Prey Preferences:** Highly favors prey larger than 15 kg, which makes up about 60% of its diet.
  - Primary targets: Capybaras, caimans (alligators), collared peccaries, and tapirs.
  - Secondary targets: Fish, turtles, armadillos, and sloths.
- **Hunting Strategy:** A solitary ambush predator. It frequently hunts in or around water, diving into rivers to drag caimans onto the banks.
- **The Killing Bite:** Instead of the typical throat-strangulation used by other big cats, the jaguar often bites directly through the temporal bone of the skull, piercing the brain for an instant kill.

## Interpretation and Comparisons

The jaguar's diet is incredibly unique due to its inclusion of heavily armored reptiles like turtles and caimans. Its specialized killing bite evolved specifically to penetrate the thick scales and shells of these aquatic prey items, showcasing an evolutionary path totally distinct from the ungulate-hunting tigers and lions.
