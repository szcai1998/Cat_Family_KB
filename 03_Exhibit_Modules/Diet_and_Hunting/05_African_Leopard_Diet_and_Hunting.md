# African Leopard – Diet and Hunting

## Overview

> A master of silence that turns the treetops into a grisly pantry.

The dietary ecology of the African Leopard (_Panthera pardus pardus_) is characterized by an incredibly broad palate and a unique strategy for protecting its hard-earned kills from larger scavengers.

## Key Information

- **Diet Composition:** Highly generalized carnivore. Documented hunting over 90 different species.
- **Prey Preferences:** Strongly prefers medium-sized ungulates in the 10–40 kg range.
  - Primary targets: Impalas, bushbucks, and duikers.
  - Secondary targets: Primates (baboons and monkeys), rodents, birds, and sometimes even insects.
- **Hunting Strategy:** Solitary ambush predator.
- **Caching:** Leopards frequently drag their kills high up into the branches of trees to prevent them from being stolen by lions and hyenas. They are capable of hauling carcasses up to 3 times their own body weight vertically into the canopy.

## Interpretation and Comparisons

While lions target massive prey for high reward (accepting high risk of injury), the solitary leopard calculates that an injury means starvation. By targeting medium-sized prey, the leopard minimizes risk. Its arboreal (tree-climbing) caching behavior is a direct evolutionary response to living in an ecosystem dominated by larger, stronger predators.
