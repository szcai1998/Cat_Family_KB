# Bengal Tiger – Range and Habitat

## Overview

> The undisputed lord of the Indian jungle, moving like liquid fire through the tall elephant grass.

The Bengal Tiger (_Panthera tigris tigris_) is the most numerous tiger subspecies and the iconic apex predator of the Indian subcontinent. Its habitat is characterized by dense vegetation and abundant water sources.

## Key Information

- **Historic Range:** Spanned the entirety of the Indian subcontinent, from the foothills of the Himalayas down to the southern tip of India.
- **Current Range:** Highly fragmented. Endemic to India, Nepal, Bhutan, and Bangladesh. India holds roughly 75% of the world's wild tiger population.
- **Habitat Type:** Extremely varied within its region. They thrive in tropical rainforests, deciduous forests, tall grasslands, and the unique mangrove swamps of the Sundarbans.
- **Home Range:** Because prey is relatively dense, a male's territory averages 267–300 km², while a female's is much smaller at 60–70 km².

## Interpretation and Comparisons

Compared to the Siberian tiger, which needs up to 1,000 km² due to the barren taiga, the Bengal tiger can survive in much smaller territories. The sheer density of deer and wild boar in Indian national parks allows Bengal tigers to maintain the highest population densities of any tiger subspecies in the world.
