# African Leopard – Behavior and Ecology

## Overview

> An elusive, solitary phantom that actively avoids the spotlight and conflict.

The behavioral ecology of the African Leopard (_Panthera pardus pardus_) revolves entirely around stealth. To survive alongside larger apex predators, the leopard must remain unseen.

## Key Information

- **Social Structure:** Strictly solitary and highly territorial.
- **Mating System:** Polygynous. Males fiercely defend large territories that encompass the smaller ranges of several females.
- **Communication:** Leopards use a rasping, rhythmic vocalization that sounds remarkably like sawing coarse wood. They also use scent marking and scratching trees to communicate.
- **Activity Pattern:** Heavily nocturnal (active at night) and crepuscular (active at dawn and dusk). They spend most of the hot daylight hours resting hidden in thickets or draped over tree branches.

## Interpretation and Comparisons

Leopards employ "spatial and temporal avoidance" to coexist with lions. If lions are hunting in an area during the early evening, a leopard will shift its hunting to the deep night or move to denser bush where lions struggle to hunt. This behavioral flexibility is key to their widespread survival.
