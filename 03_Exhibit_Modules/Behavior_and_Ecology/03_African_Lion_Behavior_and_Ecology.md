# African Lion – Behavior and Ecology

## Overview

> The only wild feline to form complex, matriarchal societies, turning the solitary cat paradigm on its head.

The most unique aspect of the African Lion (_Panthera leo_) is its social structure. Living in prides changes every aspect of their behavioral ecology, from cub rearing to territorial defense.

## Key Information

- **Social Structure:** Highly social. Prides consist of a core group of related females (sisters, mothers, aunts), their dependent cubs, and a coalition of 1–4 adult males who hold tenure over the pride.
- **Mating System:** Polygynous (males mate with all pride females), but frequently promiscuous (females may mate with multiple coalition males to confuse paternity and prevent infanticide).
- **Communication:** Vocalization is vital. They utilize a deafening roar—audible up to 8 km (5 miles) away—to declare territory and locate lost pride members.
- **Cub Rearing:** Lionesses exhibit allonursing (nursing cubs that are not their own), drastically increasing the survival rate of the pride's youth.

## Interpretation and Comparisons

Why did lions evolve to be social while tigers and leopards remain solitary? The open savannah offers no place to hide kills. A solitary cat on the plains would constantly lose its hard-earned prey to hyenas and wild dogs. By forming prides, lions can aggressively defend their kills and their territory in a hyper-competitive ecosystem.
