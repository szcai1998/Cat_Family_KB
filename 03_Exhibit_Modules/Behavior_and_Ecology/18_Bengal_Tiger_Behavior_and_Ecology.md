# Bengal Tiger – Behavior and Ecology

## Overview

> A water-loving apex predator whose roar can freeze the blood of the entire jungle.

The behavioral ecology of the Bengal Tiger (_Panthera tigris tigris_) is a complex mix of fierce territoriality and a surprising affinity for water.

## Key Information

- **Social Structure:** Solitary. The only sustained social bond is between a mother and her cubs, which stay with her for roughly two years.
- **Mating System:** Polygynous. A dominant male's territory encompasses the ranges of 3 to 5 females. Males will fiercely, and often fatally, defend this territory against nomadic rivals.
- **Water Affinity:** Unlike most cats, Bengal tigers love the water. They have partially webbed toes and are frequently found partially submerged in lakes or rivers to cool down during the intense Indian summers.
- **Communication:** They rely on deep, guttural roars to claim territory, alongside physical scent marking (scratching trees and spraying urine).

## Interpretation and Comparisons

Tigers and jaguars are the only big cats that are truly comfortable in the water. In the Sundarbans (a vast mangrove forest spanning India and Bangladesh), Bengal tigers have adapted to live an almost amphibious lifestyle, swimming for miles between islands in search of prey.
