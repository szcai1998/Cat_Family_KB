# Siberian Tiger – Conservation

## Overview

> Once hunted to the very brink of extinction, the survival of the Siberian Tiger relies on a fragile balance between anti-poaching patrols and the protection of the vast Russian taiga.

The conservation of the Siberian Tiger (_Panthera tigris altaica_) is one of the most high-stakes wildlife protection efforts in the world. Despite recovering from a critical bottleneck, their future remains precarious.

## Key Information

- **IUCN Status:** Endangered (EN).
- **Estimated Population:** Approximately 756 individuals remain in the wild (based on recent Russian census data).
- **Major Threats:**
  - **Poaching:** Targeted for the illegal wildlife trade (bones and body parts used in traditional medicine).
  - **Habitat Degradation:** Logging of the Korean pine forests destroys the ecosystem that supports their prey.
  - **Prey Depletion:** Overhunting of wild boar and deer by humans leaves tigers starving and forces them into conflict with local communities.
- **Genetic Bottleneck:** The population crashed to just 20-30 individuals in the 1940s, leaving the modern population with dangerously low genetic diversity.

## Interpretation and Comparisons

While the Siberian tiger population has stabilized significantly compared to the mid-20th century, they face a unique threat compared to African Big Cats: they require an enormous amount of contiguous (connected) forest. Without massive, unbroken tracts of wilderness, the population cannot expand.
