# Bengal Tiger – Conservation

## Overview

> An icon of Indian wildlife, fighting for space in one of the most densely populated countries on Earth.

The conservation of the Bengal Tiger (_Panthera tigris tigris_) is one of the most heavily funded and closely monitored wildlife initiatives in history (Project Tiger).

## Key Information

- **IUCN Status:** Endangered (EN).
- **Estimated Population:** Approximately 3,167 to 3,682 individuals remain in India (as of the 2022 census), making it the most numerous tiger subspecies.
- **Major Threats:**
  - **Habitat Fragmentation:** India’s rapid industrial and agricultural expansion is turning tiger reserves into isolated genetic islands.
  - **Poaching:** Tigers are still heavily targeted for the international illegal wildlife trade, where their bones and skins are highly valued.
  - **Human-Wildlife Conflict:** As tiger populations recover and disperse from overflowing reserves, they frequently wander into villages, leading to tragic conflicts, livestock predation, and retaliatory killings.

## Interpretation and Comparisons

India’s "Project Tiger" is considered one of the greatest conservation success stories of the modern era. While other tiger subspecies (like the South China or Sumatran tiger) face imminent extinction, the Bengal tiger population is actually growing. However, this success has created a new problem: India is running out of wilderness to house its booming tiger population.
