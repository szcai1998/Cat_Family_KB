# Jaguar – Conservation

## Overview

> As the Amazon burns and fragments, the jaguar is being pushed into ever-shrinking islands of forest.

The conservation of the Jaguar (_Panthera onca_) is intimately tied to the preservation of the Amazon rainforest and the Pantanal wetlands. Protecting the jaguar inherently protects thousands of other species in its ecosystem.

## Key Information

- **IUCN Status:** Near Threatened (NT).
- **Estimated Population:** Roughly 173,000 individuals remain, but populations outside the Amazon basin are highly fragmented and endangered.
- **Major Threats:**
  - **Deforestation:** Agricultural expansion (cattle ranching and soy farming) is rapidly destroying their habitat.
  - **Retaliatory Killings:** As their natural habitat shrinks, jaguars frequently prey on cattle, leading ranchers to hunt them in retaliation.
  - **Illegal Wildlife Trade:** There is a growing demand for jaguar fangs, bones, and skins in Asian markets, acting as substitutes for tiger parts.

## Interpretation and Comparisons

The jaguar's situation is unique because it spans a massive number of countries with varying conservation laws. A major initiative, the "Jaguar Corridor Initiative," aims to connect fragmented populations from Mexico to Argentina, allowing genetic flow and preventing local extinctions—a strategy highly distinct from the localized, fortress-conservation required for the Asiatic Lion.
