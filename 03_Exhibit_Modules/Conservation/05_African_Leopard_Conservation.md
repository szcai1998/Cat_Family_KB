# African Leopard – Conservation

## Overview

> Despite being the most adaptable big cat, the leopard is quietly disappearing from the edges of its vast kingdom.

Because the African Leopard (_Panthera pardus pardus_) is secretive and historically widespread, its severe regional declines have often gone unnoticed until it was too late.

## Key Information

- **IUCN Status:** Vulnerable (VU).
- **Estimated Population:** Total numbers are unknown, but the population is categorized as decreasing across the continent.
- **Major Threats:**
  - **Habitat Conversion:** As agriculture expands, the thickets and forests leopards need for cover are cleared.
  - **Bushmeat Trade:** Widespread snaring and hunting of medium-sized ungulates by humans deprives leopards of their natural prey base.
  - **Poaching:** Heavily targeted for their beautiful, highly prized skins, which are used in traditional ceremonies and the international illegal wildlife trade.
  - **Trophy Hunting:** Poorly managed legal hunting quotas in some regions have historically put unsustainable pressure on local populations.

## Interpretation and Comparisons

While lions and tigers face high-profile, coordinated conservation campaigns, the leopard suffers from a lack of reliable data. Because they are so elusive, it is incredibly difficult to count them. Consequently, they are often extirpated (locally driven extinct) from an area before researchers even realize the population was in danger.
