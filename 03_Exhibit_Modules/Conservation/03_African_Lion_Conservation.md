# African Lion – Conservation

## Overview

> A silent crisis is unfolding across the savannah; the King of the Beasts is rapidly disappearing from its throne.

The conservation status of the African Lion (_Panthera leo_) is one of the most alarming in the feline world. Because they are so visible in major tourist parks, the severity of their continent-wide decline is often overlooked by the public.

## Key Information

- **IUCN Status:** Vulnerable (VU).
- **Estimated Population:** Approximately 20,000–25,000 mature individuals remain in the wild. Populations have plummeted by over 40% in just two decades.
- **Major Threats:**
  - **Human-Wildlife Conflict:** Retaliatory killings by pastoralists using poisoned carcasses after lions prey on unprotected livestock.
  - **Habitat Fragmentation:** Rapid expansion of agriculture is cutting off historic migration corridors, isolating prides into genetic bottlenecks.
  - **Poaching:** Increasingly targeted for the illegal wildlife trade, where lion bones are substituted for tiger bones in traditional medicines.
  - **Prey Depletion:** Rampant bushmeat poaching leaves lions with nothing to eat, driving them toward livestock.

## Interpretation and Comparisons

While tigers face immense pressure from direct poaching, the primary driver of lion mortality is conflict with local herdsmen. Securing the future of the lion requires working directly with local communities to build predator-proof livestock enclosures (bomas) and providing compensation for lost cattle.
