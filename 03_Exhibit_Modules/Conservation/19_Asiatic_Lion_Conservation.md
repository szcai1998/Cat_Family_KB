# Asiatic Lion – Conservation

## Overview

> Saved from the brink of total annihilation by a single ruler, but now trapped in a dangerous genetic bottleneck.

The conservation story of the Asiatic Lion (_Panthera leo persica_) is both a miraculous success and a ticking time bomb. They represent the ultimate peril of having "all your eggs in one basket."

## Key Information

- **IUCN Status:** Endangered (EN).
- **Estimated Population:** Approximately 674 individuals (as of the 2020 census).
- **The Bottleneck:** In the early 20th century, hunting by British colonial officers and Indian royalty reduced the population to just 20 individuals. The Nawab of Junagadh banned hunting, saving the subspecies.
- **Major Threats:**
  - **Inbreeding:** Because all 674 modern lions descended from those 20 survivors, they suffer from extreme genetic impoverishment, resulting in lower fertility and weakened immune systems.
  - **Catastrophic Vulnerability:** Because 100% of the population lives in one small area, a single outbreak of Canine Distemper Virus (CDV) or Babesiosis could wipe out the entire subspecies in months.

## Interpretation and Comparisons

The only long-term solution for the Asiatic lion is relocation—establishing a second, geographically separated population to act as an "insurance policy" against disease. However, political disputes have stalled these relocation efforts for decades, leaving the lions incredibly vulnerable.
