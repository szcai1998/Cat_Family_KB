# Snow Leopard – Conservation

## Overview

> The fragile balance of the high mountains is breaking, forcing the ghost cat into deadly conflict with human herders.

The conservation of the Snow Leopard (_Panthera uncia_) is a complex issue centered around human-wildlife conflict and the rapid impacts of global climate change on alpine ecosystems.

## Key Information

- **IUCN Status:** Vulnerable (VU).
- **Estimated Population:** Between 4,000 and 7,500 mature individuals remain in the wild.
- **Major Threats:**
  - **Retaliatory Killings:** The primary threat. As wild prey (like ibex) is overhunted by humans, snow leopards turn to domestic livestock. Herders, who lose their livelihood, frequently kill the cats in retaliation.
  - **Climate Change:** Warming temperatures are causing the tree line to shift higher up the mountains, shrinking the snow leopard's alpine habitat and bringing them into competition with common leopards and wild dogs.
  - **Poaching:** Targeted for the illegal trade in skins and bones.

## Interpretation and Comparisons

Conserving the snow leopard requires a completely different approach than conserving the tiger. Most snow leopard habitat cannot be fenced or heavily patrolled. Conservationists have found success through community-based insurance programs, where herders are financially compensated for livestock lost to snow leopards, turning them from enemies into protectors.
