# Bengal Tiger – Build and Scale

## Overview

> The heavyweight champion of the tropics, possessing the muscle density to break a bull’s neck with a single swipe.

The physical morphology of the Bengal Tiger (_Panthera tigris tigris_) is designed for raw, solitary power. While slightly smaller on average than the Siberian tiger, it is far more heavily muscled relative to its frame.

## Key Information

- **Mass and Dimensions:** Males weigh 180–258 kg (397–569 lbs), while females weigh 100–160 kg (220–350 lbs). They measure roughly 2.7 to 3.1 meters in length.
- **Skull Morphometrics:** Features a massive skull with a highly pronounced sagittal crest, which anchors the temporalis muscles responsible for their crushing bite.
- **Bite Force:** Generates approximately 1,050 PSI, sufficient to shatter the vertebrae of large prey.
- **Forelimbs:** The bones of the forelimbs are incredibly thick and heavily muscled, giving them the ability to swat with enough concussive force to stun or kill.

## Interpretation and Comparisons

The Bengal tiger lacks the thick winter coat and belly fat of the Siberian tiger, resulting in a sleeker, more visibly muscular appearance. Their build is optimized for short, explosive bursts of speed in dense vegetation, rather than long-distance endurance.
